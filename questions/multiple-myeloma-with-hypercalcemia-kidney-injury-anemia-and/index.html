USMLE Step 1 practice question: Multiple myeloma with hypercalcemia, kidney injury, anemia and lytic lesions: clonal plasma cells in the marrow producing a monoclonal protein

USMLE Step 1Hematology/OncologyPathology

A 68-year-old man comes to the physician because of back pain for 3 months, fatigue, constipation and increased thirst. Serum calcium is 12.4 mg/dL, creatinine is 2.3 mg/dL, hemoglobin is 8.9 g/dL, total protein is 9.4 g/dL, and albumin is 3.2 g/dL. A peripheral blood smear shows red blood cells stacked like coins. A urine dipstick is negative for protein, but a 24-hour urine collection contains 3 g of protein. X-rays of the skull show multiple round, punched-out lytic lesions. Serum protein electrophoresis shows a monoclonal IgG κ spike.

Which of the following is most likely to be found on bone marrow biopsy?

  1. Myeloblasts making up more than 20% of nucleated cells
  2. Small mature lymphocytes with smudge cells
  3. Clonal plasma cells making up 40% of marrow cells
  4. Lymphocytes with hairlike projections
  5. Large binucleate cells with owl-eye nucleoli
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Clonal plasma cells making up 40% of marrow cells

Hypercalcemia, renal insufficiency, anemia and lytic bone lesions (CRAB), a gap between total protein and albumin, rouleaux and a monoclonal IgG spike indicate multiple myeloma, a malignancy of terminally differentiated plasma cells. The diagnosis requires at least 10% clonal plasma cells in the marrow (or a plasmacytoma) plus myeloma-defining events. Myeloma cells secrete IL-6, which supports their growth, and activate osteoclasts through RANKL and other factors while suppressing osteoblasts, producing punched-out lytic lesions, hypercalcemia and a normal alkaline phosphatase; bone scintigraphy is often negative. Free light chains (Bence Jones proteins) are filtered and precipitate in the distal tubules as casts (myeloma cast nephropathy); they are not detected by the urine dipstick, which senses albumin. Increased serum protein causes rouleaux, and reduced normal immunoglobulins predispose to infections. Myeloblasts above 20% define acute myeloid leukemia, small lymphocytes with smudge cells chronic lymphocytic leukemia, cells with hairlike projections hairy cell leukemia, and binucleate owl-eye cells (Reed-Sternberg cells) Hodgkin lymphoma.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Myeloblasts making up more than 20% of nucleated cells — More than 20% myeloblasts defines acute myeloid leukemia, which causes cytopenias but not a monoclonal protein or lytic lesions.
  • B. Small mature lymphocytes with smudge cells — Mature lymphocytes with smudge cells are typical of chronic lymphocytic leukemia.
  • D. Lymphocytes with hairlike projections — Cells with hairlike projections (TRAP-positive, BRAF V600E) characterize hairy cell leukemia.
  • E. Large binucleate cells with owl-eye nucleoli — Reed-Sternberg cells are found in lymph nodes in Hodgkin lymphoma.

Key point

Recognize multiple myeloma by CRAB features and a monoclonal protein, confirmed by clonal plasma cells in the bone marrow.

Memory hook

CRAB plus an M spike: the marrow is full of plasma cells.
