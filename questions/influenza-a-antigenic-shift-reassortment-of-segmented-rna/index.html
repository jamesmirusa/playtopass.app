USMLE Step 1 practice question: Influenza A antigenic shift: reassortment of segmented RNA genomes

USMLE Step 1MicrobiologyMicrobiology

A new influenza A virus spreads to more than 70 countries within 2 months, causing severe illness in all age groups, including adults aged 20 to 40 years who were vaccinated against the seasonal strains of the previous year. Genetic analysis shows that the hemagglutinin and neuraminidase genes of the new virus are closely related to those of an avian influenza virus, whereas its remaining genes are closely related to a circulating human strain. Investigators conclude that the virus emerged in pigs that had been infected with both an avian and a human virus at the same time.

Which of the following genetic mechanisms most likely produced this new virus?

  1. Accumulation of point mutations in the hemagglutinin gene
  2. Recombination between two DNA genomes by crossing over
  3. Reassortment of RNA genome segments
  4. Phenotypic mixing of capsid proteins without genetic change
  5. Complementation of a defective virus by a helper virus
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Reassortment of RNA genome segments

Influenza A virus has a segmented genome of eight negative-sense, single-stranded RNA segments. When two different strains infect the same cell, newly assembled virions can package segments from both parents, producing a reassortant with a new combination of genes. When this introduces a hemagglutinin or neuraminidase subtype to which the human population has little or no immunity, the result is antigenic shift, which can cause a pandemic, as occurred in 1957, 1968 and 2009. Pigs are an effective mixing vessel because their respiratory epithelium carries receptors for both avian-type (α2,3-linked) and human-type (α2,6-linked) sialic acid. Antigenic drift, by contrast, results from gradual point mutations introduced by the error-prone RNA polymerase in the hemagglutinin and neuraminidase genes; it causes seasonal epidemics and is why the vaccine is updated every year. Influenza B has no significant animal reservoir and therefore undergoes drift but not shift.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Accumulation of point mutations in the hemagglutinin gene — Point mutations in hemagglutinin cause antigenic drift, which produces gradual change and seasonal epidemics rather than a pandemic.
  • B. Recombination between two DNA genomes by crossing over — Crossing over between DNA genomes is recombination; influenza has an RNA genome that exchanges whole segments.
  • D. Phenotypic mixing of capsid proteins without genetic change — Phenotypic mixing changes the coat of progeny transiently without changing their genes, so it cannot create a stable new strain.
  • E. Complementation of a defective virus by a helper virus — Complementation lets a defective virus replicate using proteins from a helper virus, but the genome of the progeny is unchanged.

Key point

Distinguish antigenic shift by reassortment of segmented genomes from antigenic drift

Memory hook

Bird genes for HA and NA, human genes for the rest, mixed in a pig: segments were shuffled, and nobody is immune.
