USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Severe obstructive sleep apnea: continuous positive airway pressure as first-line therapy

USMLE Step 2 CKRespiratoryInternal Medicine

A 52-year-old man comes to the physician because his wife says he snores loudly and stops breathing for several seconds many times each night. He wakes unrefreshed, has morning headaches and fell asleep at a traffic light last month. His Epworth Sleepiness Scale score is 15. He drinks two beers most evenings. His BMI is 36 kg/m², neck circumference is 46 cm (18 in), and blood pressure is 148/92 mm Hg. Examination shows a crowded oropharynx. A home sleep apnea test shows an apnea-hypopnea index of 38 events per hour with oxygen saturation nadir of 79%.

Which of the following is the most appropriate initial treatment?

  1. Zolpidem at bedtime
  2. Continuous positive airway pressure
  3. Modafinil each morning
  4. Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty
  5. Supplemental oxygen during sleep alone
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Continuous positive airway pressure

Loud snoring, witnessed apneas, daytime sleepiness, obesity, a large neck and an apnea-hypopnea index of 30 or more events per hour establish severe obstructive sleep apnea (15 to 29 is moderate and 5 to 14 is mild). Repeated collapse of the pharyngeal airway during sleep causes intermittent hypoxemia, sympathetic surges and fragmented sleep, which lead to sleepiness, motor vehicle crashes, hypertension and atrial fibrillation. Positive airway pressure is first-line treatment: it splints the airway open, abolishes apneas and improves sleepiness, quality of life and blood pressure, with benefit proportional to nightly use. Weight loss, avoiding alcohol and sedatives near bedtime and positional therapy are added; tirzepatide is approved for moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity. Mandibular advancement devices suit mild to moderate disease or intolerance of positive airway pressure, and hypoglossal nerve stimulation or upper airway surgery is considered after failure of positive airway pressure. He should be counseled not to drive while drowsy.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Zolpidem at bedtime — Sedative-hypnotics relax pharyngeal muscles and blunt arousal, worsening apneas and hypoxemia.
  • C. Modafinil each morning — Modafinil treats residual sleepiness only in patients already using positive airway pressure adequately; it does not treat the apneas.
  • D. Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty — Pharyngeal surgery has modest and unpredictable success and is reserved for selected patients who fail positive airway pressure.
  • E. Supplemental oxygen during sleep alone — Oxygen alone may improve saturation but does not prevent airway collapse or arousals and can prolong apneas.

Key point

Start continuous positive airway pressure as first-line treatment for moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea

Memory hook

AHI 38, dozing at red lights: hold the airway open with air pressure every night.
