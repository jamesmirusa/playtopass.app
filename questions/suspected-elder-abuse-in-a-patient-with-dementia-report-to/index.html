USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Suspected elder abuse in a patient with dementia: report to adult protective services

USMLE Step 2 CKEthicsEthics

An 82-year-old woman with moderate Alzheimer disease is brought to the clinic by her son, who is her only caregiver. She has lost 6 kg (13 lb) in 4 months. Examination shows bruises of different colors on both upper arms in a grip pattern, a healing burn on the dorsum of the hand and poor hygiene. She cannot say how the injuries happened and becomes quiet when her son enters the room. The son says she falls often and that he is too busy to cook. She is oriented only to person.

In addition to treating her injuries, which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

  1. Report the findings to adult protective services
  2. Ask the patient for permission before taking any action
  3. Confront the son and ask him to explain each injury
  4. Arrange home physical therapy for fall prevention
  5. Schedule a follow-up visit in 2 weeks to reassess
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Report the findings to adult protective services

Patterned bruises of different ages, a burn, weight loss, poor hygiene and fearful behavior in a dependent older adult with dementia suggest physical abuse and neglect by a caregiver. Physicians should report reasonable suspicion of elder abuse to adult protective services, which is mandatory in most US states, and should make sure the patient is safe, admitting her if returning home is unsafe; proof is not required. She lacks decision-making capacity, so her permission cannot be relied on, and in states that allow competent adults to decline, capacity would still be needed. Confronting the suspected abuser can escalate the danger and does not replace reporting, and simply arranging therapy or follow-up leaves her at risk.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Ask the patient for permission before taking any action — She lacks capacity, and a report does not depend on her permission when abuse is suspected.
  • C. Confront the son and ask him to explain each injury — Confronting the suspected abuser may increase danger and does not protect her.
  • D. Arrange home physical therapy for fall prevention — Fall prevention ignores injury patterns that point to abuse.
  • E. Schedule a follow-up visit in 2 weeks to reassess — Waiting 2 weeks leaves a vulnerable adult in an unsafe situation.

Key point

Recognize elder abuse and neglect and report reasonable suspicion to adult protective services

Memory hook

Grip-mark bruises, a burn and weight loss in a dementia patient: report to adult protective services now.
