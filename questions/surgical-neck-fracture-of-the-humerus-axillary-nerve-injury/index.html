USMLE Step 1 practice question: Surgical neck fracture of the humerus: axillary nerve injury with deltoid weakness and lateral shoulder numbness

USMLE Step 1MusculoskeletalAnatomy

A 71-year-old woman comes to the emergency department after she tripped on a curb and fell onto her outstretched right arm. She has severe pain in the right shoulder. An x-ray shows a fracture through the surgical neck of the right humerus. After the arm is immobilized, examination shows loss of sensation over a small area of skin on the lateral aspect of the upper arm, just below the shoulder. Two weeks later, she cannot abduct the arm from 15 to 90 degrees, and external rotation is weak. The deltoid appears flattened.

Which of the following nerves was most likely injured?

  1. Suprascapular nerve
  2. Musculocutaneous nerve
  3. Radial nerve
  4. Long thoracic nerve
  5. Axillary nerve
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: E. Axillary nerve

The axillary nerve, from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus (C5–C6), passes through the quadrangular space with the posterior circumflex humeral artery and winds around the surgical neck of the humerus, where it is vulnerable to fractures and to anterior dislocation of the shoulder. It supplies the deltoid, which abducts the arm from about 15 to 90 degrees, and the teres minor, an external rotator, and its superior lateral cutaneous branch supplies the skin over the lower deltoid (the "regimental badge" area). Injury causes weak abduction, weak external rotation, a flattened shoulder contour from deltoid atrophy and numbness over the lateral shoulder. The suprascapular nerve supplies supraspinatus (initial 15° of abduction) and infraspinatus, the musculocutaneous nerve supplies elbow flexors and lateral forearm skin, the radial nerve is injured by midshaft fractures causing wrist drop, and long thoracic nerve injury causes winging of the scapula.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Suprascapular nerve — Suprascapular injury weakens the first 15° of abduction and external rotation without deltoid flattening.
  • B. Musculocutaneous nerve — Musculocutaneous injury weakens elbow flexion and numbs the lateral forearm.
  • C. Radial nerve — Radial nerve injury from a midshaft fracture causes wrist drop.
  • D. Long thoracic nerve — Long thoracic nerve injury causes winging of the scapula.

Key point

Link surgical neck fracture of the humerus with axillary nerve injury and its motor and sensory deficits

Memory hook

Fall, surgical neck fracture, numb badge patch, can't abduct past 15°, flat deltoid: axillary nerve.
