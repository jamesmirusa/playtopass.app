USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Cross-sectional study: exposure and outcome measured at one time give prevalence but not temporality

USMLE Step 2 CKBiostatisticsEpidemiology

A health department mails a single questionnaire to 10,000 randomly selected adults in a city. The survey asks about current smartphone use at bedtime and current symptoms of insomnia. Analysis shows that 31% of respondents report insomnia and that insomnia is more common among those who use a smartphone in bed. No participants are contacted again.

Which of the following can most validly be determined from this study?

  1. Incidence of insomnia
  2. Prevalence of insomnia
  3. Relative risk of insomnia from smartphone use
  4. Whether smartphone use preceded insomnia
  5. Median time to onset of insomnia
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Prevalence of insomnia

This is a cross-sectional study: exposure and outcome are measured at the same point in time in a sample of the population, like a snapshot. Its natural measure is prevalence (the proportion with the condition at that time), and it can show associations, usually expressed as a prevalence ratio or prevalence odds ratio. Because exposure and outcome are measured together, it cannot establish which came first; people with insomnia might use their phones in bed because they cannot sleep. Incidence, relative risk and time to onset require following people free of the outcome over time, as in a cohort study.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Incidence of insomnia — Incidence requires following people without insomnia over time to count new cases.
  • C. Relative risk of insomnia from smartphone use — Relative risk needs a cohort followed from exposure to outcome.
  • D. Whether smartphone use preceded insomnia — Measuring exposure and outcome simultaneously cannot show which came first.
  • E. Median time to onset of insomnia — Time to onset requires longitudinal follow-up.

Key point

Identify a cross-sectional study and its main measure, prevalence, and its inability to show temporality

Memory hook

One survey, one moment: a snapshot gives prevalence, not cause or timing.
