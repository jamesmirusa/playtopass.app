USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Competent patient with ALS requests withdrawal of mechanical ventilation: honor the request with palliative sedation

USMLE Step 2 CKEthicsEthics

A 62-year-old man with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis has been dependent on mechanical ventilation through a tracheostomy for 8 months. He communicates reliably with an eye-gaze device. Over the past 4 weeks, he has repeatedly and consistently asked his physicians to discontinue the ventilator, saying that his quality of life is no longer acceptable to him. A psychiatrist found no depression or delirium and confirmed that he understands that stopping ventilation will lead to his death within hours. He has no advance directive. His wife tearfully asks the team to continue ventilation because she is not ready to lose him. The palliative care team has met with the family twice.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

  1. Arrange ventilator withdrawal with sedation and analgesia for comfort
  2. Continue ventilation because withdrawal would be physician-assisted suicide
  3. Continue ventilation until his wife agrees
  4. Obtain a court order before withdrawing the ventilator
  5. Transfer him to a long-term ventilator facility
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Arrange ventilator withdrawal with sedation and analgesia for comfort

A patient with decision-making capacity has the right to refuse any treatment, including life-sustaining treatment that has already been started. Ethically and legally in the United States, withdrawing a treatment is equivalent to never starting it, and death after ventilator withdrawal results from the underlying disease, not from the physician's act. It therefore differs from physician-assisted suicide or euthanasia, in which a drug is given to cause death. His request is informed, consistent over time and made with capacity, as confirmed by psychiatric evaluation, so the team should honor it and plan a compassionate withdrawal with opioids and sedatives titrated to relieve dyspnea and distress (palliative sedation), supported by palliative care, chaplaincy and family meetings. A family member's wishes do not override the decision of a competent patient, although the team should continue to support his wife. Court involvement is unnecessary when a patient with capacity refuses treatment, and an ethics consultation can help resolve conflict but does not replace the patient's decision. An individual clinician with a moral objection may step aside but must ensure that others carry out the patient's wishes.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Continue ventilation because withdrawal would be physician-assisted suicide — Stopping an unwanted treatment allows death from the underlying disease and is not assisted suicide.
  • C. Continue ventilation until his wife agrees — A spouse cannot override the informed decision of a patient who has capacity.
  • D. Obtain a court order before withdrawing the ventilator — Courts are not needed when a patient with capacity refuses treatment.
  • E. Transfer him to a long-term ventilator facility — Transferring him to continue ventilation ignores his informed refusal.

Key point

Honor a competent patient's request to withdraw life-sustaining ventilation, providing palliative sedation for comfort

Memory hook

ALS with intact capacity, asking again and again to stop the ventilator: withdrawing equals withholding, so honor it with comfort care.
