USMLE Step 1 practice question: Testicular choriocarcinoma: markedly elevated β-hCG with gynecomastia and hyperthyroidism

USMLE Step 1ReproductivePathology

A 24-year-old man comes to the physician because of a 2-month history of breast tenderness, palpitations, heat intolerance, and a 4.5-kg (10-lb) weight loss. During the past week, he has twice coughed up streaks of blood. His pulse is 112/min, and blood pressure is 134/72 mm Hg. Examination shows a fine tremor of the outstretched hands and bilateral tender subareolar breast tissue. The thyroid gland is not enlarged. There is a firm, nontender, 2.5-cm nodule in the left testis that does not transilluminate. Laboratory studies show: β-hCG: 214,000 mIU/mL α-Fetoprotein: 4 ng/mL (N <10) Lactate dehydrogenase: 690 U/L (N 100–250) TSH: <0.01 μU/mL Free thyroxine: 3.1 ng/dL (N 0.8–1.8) Scrotal ultrasonography shows a heterogeneous intratesticular mass. A chest x-ray shows multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules. A radical inguinal orchiectomy is performed.

Histologic examination of the testicular mass is most likely to show which of the following?

  1. Sheets of uniform cells with clear cytoplasm separated by lymphocyte-rich septa
  2. Glomerulus-like structures with a central vessel and eosinophilic hyaline globules
  3. Polygonal cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm containing rod-shaped crystalloids
  4. Mature and immature tissues derived from more than one germ cell layer
  5. Malignant cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblasts without chorionic villi
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: E. Malignant cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblasts without chorionic villi

This young man has a testicular mass, an extremely high serum β-hCG with a normal AFP, early hematogenous metastases to the lungs (hemoptysis, multiple pulmonary nodules), gynecomastia, and hyperthyroidism. This presentation is characteristic of choriocarcinoma, the most aggressive nonseminomatous germ cell tumor.

Choriocarcinoma consists of malignant trophoblastic cells—mononuclear cytotrophoblasts and multinucleated syncytiotrophoblasts—arranged in sheets without chorionic villi, typically with extensive hemorrhage and necrosis. Syncytiotrophoblasts produce large amounts of hCG. Because hCG shares an identical α subunit with TSH, LH, and FSH and has a β subunit structurally similar to those of LH and TSH, very high concentrations stimulate the TSH receptor (causing hyperthyroidism with suppressed TSH) and the LH receptor on Leydig cells, increasing testicular estradiol production (aided by hCG-stimulated aromatase activity) and causing gynecomastia. Choriocarcinoma invades blood vessels early and spreads hematogenously to the lungs, liver, and brain, often while the primary tumor is still small; hemorrhagic lung metastases can cause hemoptysis.

Serum tumor markers guide the diagnosis, staging, and monitoring of testicular germ cell tumors: AFP is elevated in yolk sac tumor (and mixed tumors containing it) but never in pure seminoma or pure choriocarcinoma; hCG is massively elevated in choriocarcinoma and can be mildly elevated in seminoma containing syncytiotrophoblastic giant cells; LDH reflects tumor burden. Most choriocarcinomas occur as a component of mixed germ cell tumors.

High-yield summary: Testicular tumor markers—seminoma: AFP normal, hCG normal or mildly ↑, PLAP; yolk sac tumor: ↑↑AFP, Schiller-Duval bodies; choriocarcinoma: ↑↑↑hCG, trophoblasts without villi, early lung metastases, gynecomastia, and hyperthyroidism; teratoma: markers usually normal; Leydig cell tumor: Reinke crystals, estrogen excess.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Sheets of uniform cells with clear cytoplasm separated by lymphocyte-rich septa — Sheets of uniform clear cells with lymphocyte-rich fibrous septa describe seminoma, which has a normal AFP and at most a mildly elevated hCG, grows relatively slowly, and spreads first to retroperitoneal lymph nodes.
  • B. Glomerulus-like structures with a central vessel and eosinophilic hyaline globules — Schiller-Duval bodies (glomerulus-like structures) and hyaline globules characterize yolk sac tumor, which markedly elevates AFP; this patient's AFP is normal.
  • C. Polygonal cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm containing rod-shaped crystalloids — Reinke crystals in polygonal eosinophilic cells characterize Leydig cell tumors, which may cause gynecomastia through estrogen production but do not secrete hCG or cause hyperthyroidism and pulmonary metastases.
  • D. Mature and immature tissues derived from more than one germ cell layer — Tissues from multiple germ layers define teratoma; tumor markers are usually normal, and teratoma does not explain this extreme hCG elevation, hyperthyroidism, and early hematogenous spread.

Key point

Testicular choriocarcinoma, composed of cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblasts without villi, produces very high hCG that can cause gynecomastia and hyperthyroidism, and it spreads early to the lungs.

Memory hook

Choriocarcinoma: hCG through the roof — it impersonates TSH (thyrotoxicosis) and LH (gynecomastia), and races through blood to the lungs. Trophoblasts, no villi.
