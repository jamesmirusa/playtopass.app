USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Suspected subacute infective endocarditis: three blood culture sets before antibiotics, then echocardiography

USMLE Step 2 CKCardiovascularInternal Medicine

A 44-year-old man comes to the physician because of 3 weeks of fatigue, night sweats, low-grade fevers, and a 3-kg (6.6-lb) weight loss. Seven weeks ago, he had a dental extraction. He has known mitral valve prolapse with mild regurgitation. He does not use injection drugs. Temperature is 38.2°C (100.8°F), pulse is 94/min, respirations are 16/min, and blood pressure is 126/74 mm Hg. He appears tired but not toxic. A grade 3/6 holosystolic murmur is heard at the apex, louder than documented 1 year ago. There are splinter hemorrhages under two fingernails and a few painless erythematous macules on the palms. Leukocyte count is 11,800/mm³, hemoglobin is 11.2 g/dL, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate is 68 mm/h. No antibiotics have been given.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

  1. Start IV vancomycin and ceftriaxone now, then draw blood cultures
  2. Draw one set of blood cultures and start oral amoxicillin
  3. Transesophageal echocardiography before any cultures are drawn
  4. Referral for urgent mitral valve repair or replacement
  5. Three sets of blood cultures, then transthoracic echocardiography
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: E. Three sets of blood cultures, then transthoracic echocardiography

Subacute fever with constitutional symptoms, a worsening regurgitant murmur on an abnormal valve, splinter hemorrhages, and Janeway lesions weeks after dental work point to infective endocarditis, most likely from viridans group streptococci. The diagnosis rests on the 2023 Duke-ISCVID criteria, whose two pillars are microbiology (typical organisms in repeated blood cultures) and imaging evidence of valvular involvement. In a hemodynamically stable patient with a subacute course, at least three sets of blood cultures from separate venipunctures should be drawn before any antibiotic, ideally spaced over time (the first and last at least 1 hour apart). Even a single dose of an antibiotic can sterilize cultures for days and leave the organism and its susceptibilities unknown, so in a stable patient empiric therapy waits until the cultures are drawn.

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) is the first imaging test. Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) follows when TTE is nondiagnostic, when suspicion stays high despite a normal TTE, with prosthetic valves or intracardiac devices, or to look for complications such as a perivalvular abscess. If the patient were septic or unstable, the three culture sets would be drawn within minutes and empiric antibiotics started immediately afterward.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Start IV vancomycin and ceftriaxone now, then draw blood cultures — Starting antibiotics before cultures in a stable patient risks culture-negative endocarditis and the loss of organism-directed therapy.
  • B. Draw one set of blood cultures and start oral amoxicillin — One culture set is insufficient for the Duke microbiologic criterion, and oral amoxicillin is not adequate initial therapy for endocarditis.
  • C. Transesophageal echocardiography before any cultures are drawn — TEE is not the first imaging test here and never replaces blood cultures; TTE comes first, with TEE if needed.
  • D. Referral for urgent mitral valve repair or replacement — Surgery is indicated for complications such as heart failure, abscess, or uncontrolled infection, none of which is present, and the diagnosis is not yet established.

Key point

In stable suspected infective endocarditis, obtain at least three sets of blood cultures before antibiotics, followed by transthoracic echocardiography.

Memory hook

Culture before you cure: three sets, separate sticks, then the echo. Antibiotics first can blind the diagnosis for days.
