USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Vaccination before elective splenectomy: pneumococcal, meningococcal ACWY and B, and Hib at least 2 weeks before

USMLE Step 2 CKImmunologySurgery

A 27-year-old woman with chronic immune thrombocytopenia that has not responded to glucocorticoids, intravenous immune globulin or a thrombopoietin receptor agonist is scheduled for elective laparoscopic splenectomy in 1 month. Her platelet count is 18,000/mm³. She received all routine childhood vaccines, including Haemophilus influenzae type b, and her most recent influenza vaccine was 2 months ago. She has never received pneumococcal or meningococcal vaccines in adulthood.

Which of the following is the most appropriate plan regarding vaccination?

  1. Give pneumococcal vaccine only, on the day of surgery
  2. Pneumococcal, meningococcal and Hib vaccines at least 2 weeks before surgery
  3. Give no vaccines until platelet count normalizes after surgery
  4. Give daily oral penicillin for life instead of vaccines
  5. Give meningococcal vaccines only, 3 months after surgery
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Pneumococcal, meningococcal and Hib vaccines at least 2 weeks before surgery

The spleen filters encapsulated bacteria from the blood, and its marginal-zone B cells produce IgM against polysaccharide antigens; after splenectomy, patients are at lifelong risk of overwhelming postsplenectomy infection, most often from Streptococcus pneumoniae, Neisseria meningitidis and Haemophilus influenzae type b, which can progress to death within hours. ACIP recommends that patients undergoing elective splenectomy receive pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV20 or PCV21, or PCV15 followed by PPSV23), two doses of MenACWY with boosters every 5 years, a MenB series, and one dose of Hib vaccine even if vaccinated in childhood, at least 14 days before surgery, when the immune response is better. If vaccines cannot be given beforehand, they are given 14 days or more after surgery. Annual influenza vaccination continues. Patients should also carry a standby antibiotic such as amoxicillin-clavulanate to take at the first sign of fever while seeking care, and they need education about the risk. Intramuscular vaccines can be given safely with platelets above about 10,000 to 20,000/mm³ using a fine needle and firm pressure.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Give pneumococcal vaccine only, on the day of surgery — Pneumococcal vaccine alone leaves meningococcal and Hib risks uncovered, and day-of-surgery timing gives a weaker response.
  • C. Give no vaccines until platelet count normalizes after surgery — Low platelet count is not a reason to withhold vaccines; delaying protection leaves her vulnerable.
  • D. Give daily oral penicillin for life instead of vaccines — Antibiotic prophylaxis is not a substitute for vaccination in adults and is used selectively, mainly in children.
  • E. Give meningococcal vaccines only, 3 months after surgery — Meningococcal vaccine alone is incomplete, and a planned splenectomy allows vaccination before surgery.

Key point

Vaccinate against encapsulated organisms at least 2 weeks before elective splenectomy

Memory hook

Spleen coming out in a month: pneumococcus, meningococcus (ACWY and B) and Hib shots at least 2 weeks before.
