USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Surrogate decision-making: advance directive and designated health care agent come first

USMLE Step 2 CKEthicsEthics

A 79-year-old man is admitted to the intensive care unit after a large left intracerebral hemorrhage. He is intubated and unresponsive, and the neurologist estimates that if he survives, he will remain severely disabled and dependent on others for all care. Three years ago, he completed an advance directive stating that he would not want mechanical ventilation or tube feeding if he were unlikely to regain the ability to recognize his family, and he appointed his niece, who lives with him, as his health care agent through a durable power of attorney for health care. His wife died 5 years ago. His son, who lives in another state and had not seen him for several years, arrives and demands that everything be done. The niece states that her uncle would not want to continue life support in this situation.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

  1. Follow the niece's decision, which is consistent with his advance directive
  2. Follow the son's wishes because he is the closest living relative
  3. Continue full life support until the son and niece reach agreement
  4. Petition the court to appoint a guardian to make decisions for him
  5. Ask the hospital ethics committee to choose the course of treatment
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Follow the niece's decision, which is consistent with his advance directive

When a patient lacks decision-making capacity, decisions are guided first by the patient's own previously expressed wishes. A written advance directive (living will) records treatment preferences, and a durable power of attorney for health care names a specific agent (health care proxy) to make decisions on the patient's behalf. A legally appointed agent takes priority over all default surrogates, including a spouse or adult children; the default order of priority (typically spouse, adult children, parents, siblings, then other relatives or close friends) is set by state law and applies only when no agent has been named (AMA Code of Medical Ethics Opinion 2.1.2). The agent should use substituted judgment, deciding as the patient would have decided, and should decide according to the patient's best interests only when the patient's wishes cannot be known or inferred.

Here, the niece is the designated agent, and her decision matches the patient's written directive, so withdrawing mechanical ventilation and providing comfort-focused care is appropriate. Disagreement from other family members does not override the patient's documented choices. The team should meet with the son, explain his father's wishes and prognosis, and offer support such as palliative care and chaplaincy; an ethics consultation may help mediate the conflict, but ethics committees advise rather than make decisions for patients. Court-appointed guardianship is a last resort when no appropriate surrogate exists or a conflict cannot be resolved.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Follow the son's wishes because he is the closest living relative — Default family surrogate hierarchies apply only when the patient has not appointed an agent; the son's wishes do not override the directive.
  • C. Continue full life support until the son and niece reach agreement — Continuing treatment that the patient explicitly refused while waiting for family consensus disregards his autonomy.
  • D. Petition the court to appoint a guardian to make decisions for him — Guardianship is a last resort when no appropriate surrogate exists; a valid agent and directive are already in place.
  • E. Ask the hospital ethics committee to choose the course of treatment — Ethics committees can help mediate disagreements but do not make treatment decisions when a valid agent exists.

Key point

For a patient who lacks capacity, the designated health care agent and the patient's advance directive take precedence over other family members' wishes.

Memory hook

The patient's own written wishes and chosen agent outrank the loudest relative; default family order applies only if no agent was named.
