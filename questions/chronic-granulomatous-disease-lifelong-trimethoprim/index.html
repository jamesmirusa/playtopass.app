USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Chronic granulomatous disease: lifelong trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and itraconazole prophylaxis

USMLE Step 2 CKImmunologyPediatrics

A 4-year-old boy is brought to the physician for follow-up after hospitalization for a liver abscess caused by Staphylococcus aureus. At age 2 years he had cervical lymphadenitis caused by Serratia marcescens, and last year he had pneumonia caused by Aspergillus fumigatus. A maternal uncle died of pneumonia in childhood. Growth is at the 10th percentile. Examination shows healed scars over the neck and hepatomegaly. Leukocyte count, lymphocyte subsets and serum immunoglobulin concentrations are normal. A dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry test shows no increase in neutrophil fluorescence after stimulation.

Which of the following is the most appropriate long-term prophylaxis for this patient?

  1. Monthly intravenous immunoglobulin
  2. Daily oral penicillin V
  3. Daily oral fluconazole alone
  4. Daily trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and itraconazole
  5. Azithromycin three times weekly alone
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Daily trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and itraconazole

Recurrent deep infections with catalase-positive organisms (Staphylococcus aureus, Serratia, Burkholderia cepacia, Nocardia, Aspergillus) and an abnormal dihydrorhodamine test indicate chronic granulomatous disease, a defect of the phagocyte NADPH oxidase, most often X-linked (CYBB, gp91phox). Phagocytes cannot generate superoxide, so ingested organisms that destroy their own hydrogen peroxide with catalase survive, and persistent inflammation produces granulomas. Standard management is lifelong antibacterial prophylaxis with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and antifungal prophylaxis with itraconazole, which is active against Aspergillus; interferon gamma is often added. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is the only established cure. Patients should avoid exposure to mulch, hay and decaying plant material because of mold spores.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Monthly intravenous immunoglobulin — Immunoglobulin replacement treats antibody deficiency; humoral immunity is normal in chronic granulomatous disease.
  • B. Daily oral penicillin V — Penicillin prophylaxis is used for asplenia and sickle cell disease to prevent pneumococcal sepsis; it does not cover the organisms seen here.
  • C. Daily oral fluconazole alone — Fluconazole has no reliable activity against Aspergillus and gives no antibacterial protection.
  • E. Azithromycin three times weekly alone — Azithromycin prophylaxis is used against Mycobacterium avium complex in advanced HIV infection.

Key point

Select antibacterial and antifungal prophylaxis for chronic granulomatous disease

Memory hook

Catalase-positive bugs keep winning: give the neutrophils cover with TMP-SMX plus itraconazole.
