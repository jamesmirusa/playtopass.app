USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Interpreting a hazard ratio and its confidence interval from a survival analysis

USMLE Step 2 CKBiostatisticsBiostatistics

A randomized trial enrolls 4200 patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and assigns them to a new drug or placebo in addition to standard therapy. Patients are followed for a median of 2.1 years, and time to the first occurrence of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure is analyzed with Kaplan-Meier curves and a Cox proportional hazards model. The primary outcome occurs in 16.3% of patients in the drug group and 21.2% in the placebo group. The hazard ratio is 0.74 (95% confidence interval 0.65 to 0.85; P <0.001).

Which of the following is the most accurate interpretation of the hazard ratio?

  1. 74% of patients taking the drug were protected from the outcome
  2. The drug reduced the absolute risk of the outcome by 26 percentage points
  3. At any time during follow-up, patients taking the drug had a 74% chance of avoiding the outcome
  4. At any given time, the instantaneous rate of the outcome was about 26% lower with the drug
  5. The result is not statistically significant because the confidence interval does not include 0
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. At any given time, the instantaneous rate of the outcome was about 26% lower with the drug

A hazard ratio compares the hazard, or instantaneous event rate among patients still at risk, in one group with that in another over the follow-up period, assuming the ratio is roughly constant over time (proportional hazards). A hazard ratio of 0.74 means that at any point during follow-up, patients taking the drug experienced the outcome at about 74% of the rate of those taking placebo, a relative reduction of about 26%. For ratio measures such as hazard ratios, relative risks and odds ratios, the line of no effect is 1; a 95% confidence interval that excludes 1, here 0.65 to 0.85, indicates statistical significance at the 5% level. A hazard ratio is a relative measure and does not give the absolute benefit; here the absolute difference in the proportion with an event is 21.2% − 16.3% = 4.9 percentage points, corresponding to a number needed to treat of about 20 over 2.1 years. Kaplan-Meier curves show the probability of remaining event-free over time, and the gap between curves visualizes the treatment effect.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 74% of patients taking the drug were protected from the outcome — A hazard ratio is not the proportion of patients protected; most patients in both groups had no event.
  • B. The drug reduced the absolute risk of the outcome by 26 percentage points — A 26% relative reduction in hazard is not a 26-percentage-point absolute reduction; the absolute difference is about 4.9 percentage points.
  • C. At any time during follow-up, patients taking the drug had a 74% chance of avoiding the outcome — The hazard ratio compares event rates between groups; it is not the probability of avoiding the outcome.
  • E. The result is not statistically significant because the confidence interval does not include 0 — For ratio measures the null value is 1, not 0; an interval excluding 1 is statistically significant.

Key point

Interpret a hazard ratio as a relative difference in instantaneous event rates, with 1 as the null value

Memory hook

Hazard ratio 0.74: events happen about a quarter less often at every moment—relative, not absolute, and 1 is the line of no effect.
