USMLE Step 1 practice question: Sleep terrors: NREM stage N3 (delta-wave) parasomnia

USMLE Step 1PsychiatryBehavioral Science

A 5-year-old boy is brought to the physician by his parents because of episodes during sleep that began 3 months ago. Two or three times each week, about 1 to 2 hours after falling asleep, he suddenly sits up in bed, screams, and appears terrified. During the episodes, he is sweaty and breathes rapidly, his heart is pounding, and his pupils are dilated. He does not respond to his parents' attempts to comfort him and seems confused if awakened. After 5 to 10 minutes, he falls back asleep, and in the morning he has no memory of the event. He has no daytime sleepiness, and his behavior at preschool is normal. His growth and development are appropriate for age. Physical and neurologic examinations show no abnormalities.

These episodes most likely arise from the stage of sleep characterized by which of the following electroencephalographic findings?

  1. Sleep spindles and K complexes
  2. High-amplitude, low-frequency delta waves
  3. Theta waves with vertex sharp waves
  4. Low-voltage, mixed-frequency waves with sawtooth waves
  5. Posterior-dominant alpha rhythm
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. High-amplitude, low-frequency delta waves

This child has sleep terrors (night terrors), a non–rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep arousal disorder. Episodes occur in the first third of the night and consist of sudden screaming with intense fear and marked autonomic arousal (tachycardia, tachypnea, diaphoresis, mydriasis). The child is difficult to awaken or console, is confused if awakened, and has amnesia for the event. Sleep terrors arise from partial arousal out of stage N3 (slow-wave sleep), which dominates the first part of the night and is most abundant in childhood. N3 is defined by high-amplitude, low-frequency (0.5–2 Hz) delta waves occupying >20% of an epoch. Other N3 parasomnias include sleepwalking (somnambulism) and confusional arousals; nocturnal enuresis also commonly occurs during NREM sleep, especially early in the night. A family history of parasomnias is common, and sleep deprivation, fever, and stress are triggers.

Sleep terrors must be distinguished from nightmares, which arise from REM sleep in the last third of the night; the child awakens fully, is easily comforted, and vividly recalls a frightening dream. Most children outgrow sleep terrors by adolescence. Management is reassurance, ensuring safety, a regular sleep schedule, and avoiding sleep deprivation; scheduled awakenings shortly before the usual time of the episodes can help. Benzodiazepines suppress N3 but are rarely needed.

High-yield summary: Sleep EEG mnemonic ‘BATS Drink Blood’—Beta (awake, eyes open), Alpha (awake, eyes closed), Theta (N1), Sleep spindles and K complexes (N2), Delta (N3), Beta-like with sawtooth waves (REM). N3 parasomnias (early night, no recall): sleep terrors, sleepwalking, (enuresis). REM (late night, recall): nightmares, REM sleep behavior disorder.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Sleep spindles and K complexes — Sleep spindles and K complexes define stage N2, which makes up the largest proportion (about 45%–55%) of total sleep in adults; bruxism is classically associated with N2, but sleep terrors are not.
  • C. Theta waves with vertex sharp waves — Theta waves with vertex sharp waves characterize stage N1, the lightest stage of sleep at the transition from wakefulness; hypnic jerks occur here, but complex arousal behaviors do not.
  • D. Low-voltage, mixed-frequency waves with sawtooth waves — Low-voltage, mixed-frequency (beta-like) activity with sawtooth waves characterizes REM sleep, during which nightmares occur—typically late in the night, with full awakening and vivid recall of the dream.
  • E. Posterior-dominant alpha rhythm — A posterior-dominant alpha rhythm is seen in a relaxed, awake person with eyes closed; it disappears at sleep onset and is not the substrate of sleep terrors.

Key point

Sleep terrors and sleepwalking are NREM parasomnias arising from stage N3 (delta-wave) sleep in the first third of the night, whereas nightmares arise from REM sleep.

Memory hook

Delta = Don't remember (night terrors, sleepwalking, early night); REM = REMember (nightmares, late night).
