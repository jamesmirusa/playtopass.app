USMLE Step 1 practice question: Allergic contact dermatitis to nickel: hapten-specific T cells sensitized by Langerhans cells (type IV hypersensitivity)

USMLE Step 1DermatologyImmunology

A 28-year-old woman comes to the physician because of an itchy rash below her navel for 1 week. She recently started wearing a new pair of jeans with a metal button. She had a similar itchy rash on her earlobes years ago after wearing inexpensive earrings. Examination shows a sharply demarcated, 3-cm erythematous plaque with tiny vesicles, oozing and scale directly beneath the button; the rest of the skin is normal. A biopsy shows intercellular edema of the epidermis (spongiosis) with a perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate. Patch testing with nickel sulfate produces an itchy, vesicular reaction at 72 hours.

Which of the following mechanisms is most likely responsible for this patient's rash?

  1. IgE-mediated degranulation of mast cells
  2. IgG antibodies against basement membrane proteins
  3. Hapten-specific T cells sensitized by antigen-presenting cells in the skin
  4. Immune complex deposition in dermal vessels
  5. Direct chemical damage to keratinocytes without immune memory
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Hapten-specific T cells sensitized by antigen-presenting cells in the skin

An itchy, vesicular, eczematous plaque confined to the site of contact with a metal object, a history of earring reactions and a positive patch test read at 48 to 72 hours indicate allergic contact dermatitis to nickel, the most common contact allergen. It is a delayed (type IV) hypersensitivity reaction. In the sensitization phase, the small nickel ion acts as a hapten, binding skin proteins to form new antigens that Langerhans cells and dermal dendritic cells carry to regional lymph nodes, where they prime hapten-specific T cells over 10 to 14 days. On re-exposure, the elicitation phase follows in 24 to 72 hours: memory CD8+ and CD4+ T cells recruited to the skin release cytokines and kill keratinocytes, producing spongiotic dermatitis. Other common allergens include poison ivy (urushiol), fragrances, preservatives and topical neomycin. Management is avoidance (for example, covering metal buttons) and topical corticosteroids. Irritant contact dermatitis, by contrast, is nonimmunologic damage that can occur on first exposure, as from frequent hand washing or detergents.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. IgE-mediated degranulation of mast cells — IgE-mediated reactions cause urticaria or anaphylaxis within minutes, not eczema appearing days after contact.
  • B. IgG antibodies against basement membrane proteins — Antibodies against basement membrane cause bullous pemphigoid, with tense blisters not limited to a contact site.
  • D. Immune complex deposition in dermal vessels — Immune complex deposition causes vasculitis with palpable purpura, not a sharply demarcated eczematous plaque.
  • E. Direct chemical damage to keratinocytes without immune memory — Irritant contact dermatitis is nonimmunologic, but the earlier earring reaction and positive patch test show immune memory.

Key point

Explain allergic contact dermatitis as a hapten-driven, T-cell–mediated type IV reaction

Memory hook

Rash exactly under the jeans button, earrings did it before, patch test positive at 72 hours: nickel-primed T cells.
