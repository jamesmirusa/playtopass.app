USMLE Step 1 practice question: Von Willebrand disease type 1: desmopressin releases von Willebrand factor from Weibel-Palade bodies

USMLE Step 1Hematology/OncologyPharmacology

A 16-year-old girl is brought to the physician before a planned wisdom tooth extraction because she has had heavy menstrual periods since menarche, frequent nosebleeds lasting more than 15 minutes and easy bruising. Her mother has similar symptoms. Examination shows scattered bruises on her shins. Laboratory studies show: Hemoglobin: 11.0 g/dL Platelet count: 260,000/mm³ Prothrombin time: normal Activated partial thromboplastin time: 39 seconds (reference 25–35) von Willebrand factor antigen: 32% (reference 50–150) von Willebrand factor activity (ristocetin cofactor): 30% Factor VIII activity: 45% A trial dose of a drug produced a threefold rise in von Willebrand factor and factor VIII levels.

The drug given most likely acts by which of the following mechanisms?

  1. Release of stored von Willebrand factor from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies
  2. Increased γ-carboxylation of hepatic clotting factors
  3. Neutralization of circulating heparin
  4. Supply of functional platelets from donors
  5. Replacement of factor IX
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Release of stored von Willebrand factor from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies

Lifelong mucocutaneous bleeding (heavy menses, prolonged epistaxis, bruising) with an autosomal dominant family pattern, a normal platelet count, a mildly prolonged aPTT and proportionally reduced von Willebrand factor antigen and activity indicate type 1 von Willebrand disease, the most common inherited bleeding disorder. Von Willebrand factor mediates adhesion of platelets to exposed subendothelial collagen through platelet glycoprotein Ib and carries and protects factor VIII in plasma; reduced levels therefore impair primary hemostasis and lower factor VIII, prolonging the aPTT. Desmopressin, a synthetic vasopressin analogue that acts on V2 receptors, releases von Willebrand factor and factor VIII stored in the Weibel-Palade bodies of endothelial cells, raising levels two- to fivefold within about 30 minutes; a test dose confirms response before procedures. Antifibrinolytics such as tranexamic acid are useful adjuncts for dental and mucosal bleeding, and von Willebrand factor–containing concentrates are used when desmopressin is ineffective or contraindicated (type 3 and some type 2 variants; it can worsen thrombocytopenia in type 2B). Fluid restriction limits the risk of hyponatremia.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Increased γ-carboxylation of hepatic clotting factors — Vitamin K supports γ-carboxylation of factors II, VII, IX and X; it does not raise von Willebrand factor.
  • C. Neutralization of circulating heparin — Protamine neutralizes heparin and has no role in von Willebrand disease.
  • D. Supply of functional platelets from donors — Platelet transfusion does not correct a deficiency of von Willebrand factor, which is the main problem here.
  • E. Replacement of factor IX — Factor IX concentrate treats hemophilia B, not von Willebrand disease.

Key point

Explain how desmopressin treats type 1 von Willebrand disease

Memory hook

Heavy periods, long nosebleeds, normal platelets, low vWF: desmopressin empties the endothelium's Weibel-Palade stores.
