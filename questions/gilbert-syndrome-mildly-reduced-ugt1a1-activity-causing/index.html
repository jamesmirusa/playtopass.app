USMLE Step 1 practice question: Gilbert syndrome: mildly reduced UGT1A1 activity causing intermittent unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia with fasting or stress

USMLE Step 1GastrointestinalBiochemistry

A 21-year-old college student comes to the student health clinic because his roommate noticed that his eyes were yellow. During final examinations he has slept little and skipped meals for several days; he recalls a similar episode last year while he had influenza. He takes no medications and drinks alcohol rarely. Examination shows mild scleral icterus; there is no hepatosplenomegaly. Serum total bilirubin concentration is 3.2 mg/dL with a direct fraction of 0.3 mg/dL. Serum aminotransferase and alkaline phosphatase activities are normal. Hemoglobin concentration, reticulocyte count, lactate dehydrogenase and haptoglobin concentrations, and a peripheral blood smear are normal.

Which of the following best explains this patient's hyperbilirubinemia?

  1. Absent bilirubin UDP-glucuronosyltransferase activity
  2. Defective canalicular excretion of conjugated bilirubin
  3. Mildly reduced bilirubin UDP-glucuronosyltransferase activity
  4. Increased destruction of red blood cells
  5. Defective hepatic reuptake of conjugated bilirubin
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Mildly reduced bilirubin UDP-glucuronosyltransferase activity

Mild, intermittent unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia triggered by fasting, sleep deprivation or illness in an otherwise healthy young adult, with normal liver enzymes and no evidence of hemolysis, is Gilbert syndrome. It affects several percent of the population and is most often caused by an extra TA repeat in the promoter of UGT1A1, reducing expression of bilirubin UDP-glucuronosyltransferase to roughly a third of normal. Fasting and stress raise bilirubin by increasing the bilirubin load and decreasing conjugation and hepatic uptake. The condition is benign and needs no treatment, but reduced UGT1A1 activity increases the toxicity of drugs cleared by glucuronidation, such as irinotecan. Crigler-Najjar syndrome type I (absent enzyme) causes severe neonatal jaundice and kernicterus; Dubin-Johnson syndrome (MRP2 defect, black liver) and Rotor syndrome (defective hepatic reuptake and storage) cause conjugated hyperbilirubinemia; and hemolysis would raise the reticulocyte count and LDH and lower haptoglobin.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Absent bilirubin UDP-glucuronosyltransferase activity — Absent enzyme activity (Crigler-Najjar type I) causes severe neonatal jaundice and kernicterus.
  • B. Defective canalicular excretion of conjugated bilirubin — Defective canalicular excretion (Dubin-Johnson) causes conjugated, not unconjugated, hyperbilirubinemia.
  • D. Increased destruction of red blood cells — Hemolysis would raise the reticulocyte count and LDH and lower haptoglobin.
  • E. Defective hepatic reuptake of conjugated bilirubin — Defective reuptake and storage (Rotor) causes conjugated hyperbilirubinemia.

Key point

Recognize Gilbert syndrome as benign unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia from reduced UGT1A1 activity

Memory hook

Yellow eyes during exam week, normal everything else: Gilbert—a lazy UGT1A1, nothing to treat.
