USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Syphilis in pregnancy with penicillin allergy: desensitization followed by benzathine penicillin G

USMLE Step 2 CKMicrobiologyObstetrics & Gynecology

A 26-year-old woman, gravida 2, para 1, at 14 weeks' gestation comes for her first prenatal visit. For 2 weeks she has had a nonpruritic rash on her trunk, palms and soles. At age 19 years she developed throat swelling, wheezing and hives within minutes of taking amoxicillin and was treated with epinephrine. Examination shows copper-colored macules on the palms and soles and generalized nontender lymphadenopathy. Fetal heart tones are present. A rapid plasma reagin titer is 1:64, and a treponemal antibody test is reactive. HIV testing is negative.

Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

  1. Penicillin desensitization followed by intramuscular benzathine penicillin G
  2. Oral doxycycline for 14 days
  3. Oral azithromycin in a single 2-g dose
  4. Intramuscular ceftriaxone daily for 10 days
  5. Deferral of treatment until after delivery, with treatment of the newborn
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Penicillin desensitization followed by intramuscular benzathine penicillin G

A diffuse rash involving the palms and soles with generalized lymphadenopathy and reactive nontreponemal and treponemal tests is secondary syphilis. Untreated infection in pregnancy can cause miscarriage, stillbirth, hydrops, preterm birth and congenital syphilis. According to CDC guidance, parenteral penicillin G is the only therapy with documented efficacy for treating the fetus and preventing congenital syphilis, so pregnant patients with a penicillin allergy must be desensitized in a monitored setting and then treated with the penicillin regimen for their stage: benzathine penicillin G, 2.4 million units intramuscularly once, for primary, secondary or early latent disease. Treatment may be followed within hours by the Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (fever, myalgia, contractions), about which patients should be warned. Titers are followed to document a fourfold decline, and sexual partners are evaluated and treated.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Oral doxycycline for 14 days — Doxycycline is an alternative in nonpregnant adults but is avoided in pregnancy and does not reliably treat the fetus.
  • C. Oral azithromycin in a single 2-g dose — Azithromycin resistance is widespread, and the drug does not reliably cross the placenta to treat the fetus.
  • D. Intramuscular ceftriaxone daily for 10 days — Ceftriaxone has limited data in pregnancy and is not accepted for preventing congenital syphilis.
  • E. Deferral of treatment until after delivery, with treatment of the newborn — Deferring therapy exposes the fetus to infection during the stage with the highest transmission risk.

Key point

Treat syphilis in a penicillin-allergic pregnant patient with desensitization and benzathine penicillin G

Memory hook

Pregnant, syphilis, penicillin anaphylaxis: no substitutes. Desensitize, then give penicillin.
