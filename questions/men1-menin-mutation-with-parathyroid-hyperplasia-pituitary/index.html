USMLE Step 1 practice question: MEN1: menin mutation with parathyroid hyperplasia, pituitary adenoma and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (gastrinoma)

USMLE Step 1EndocrineGenetics

A 38-year-old man comes to the physician because of burning epigastric pain and watery diarrhea for 6 months. Upper endoscopy shows multiple ulcers in the distal duodenum and proximal jejunum that persist despite high-dose proton pump inhibitor therapy. He passed a calcium kidney stone last year and reports decreased libido. His father had a 'tumor of the pancreas' and recurrent kidney stones. Serum calcium concentration is 11.4 mg/dL, parathyroid hormone concentration is increased, fasting serum gastrin concentration is 1200 pg/mL (N <100) with a gastric pH of 1.5, and serum prolactin concentration is 180 ng/mL. MRI of the brain shows a 9-mm pituitary adenoma.

A germline mutation in which of the following genes is most likely responsible for this patient's condition?

  1. RET
  2. VHL
  3. NF1
  4. MEN1
  5. APC
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. MEN1

Primary hyperparathyroidism, a gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome with refractory ulcers in unusual locations and diarrhea) and a prolactin-secreting pituitary adenoma, with a similarly affected father, indicate multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (the '3 Ps': parathyroid, pituitary, pancreas). MEN1 is autosomal dominant and caused by inactivating mutations of the tumor suppressor gene MEN1, which encodes menin; tumors arise after a second, somatic hit. Hyperparathyroidism from multigland hyperplasia is the most common and usually earliest feature. Pancreatic and duodenal neuroendocrine tumors include gastrinomas (often multiple, small and in the duodenum), insulinomas and nonfunctioning tumors, and they are the leading cause of death. Pituitary adenomas are most often prolactinomas. Correcting hypercalcemia can lower gastrin secretion. RET gain-of-function mutations cause MEN2A and MEN2B (medullary thyroid carcinoma, pheochromocytoma), VHL causes hemangioblastomas, renal cell carcinoma and pheochromocytoma, NF1 causes neurofibromas, and APC causes familial adenomatous polyposis.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. RET — RET mutations cause MEN2 with medullary thyroid carcinoma and pheochromocytoma, not gastrinoma or pituitary adenoma.
  • B. VHL — VHL mutations cause hemangioblastomas, renal cell carcinoma and pheochromocytoma.
  • C. NF1 — NF1 mutations cause neurofibromas, café-au-lait macules and Lisch nodules.
  • E. APC — APC mutations cause familial adenomatous polyposis with hundreds of colonic adenomas.

Key point

Recognize MEN1 (parathyroid, pituitary, pancreas) and its menin tumor suppressor gene

Memory hook

Stones, ulcers and a prolactinoma in one family: the 3 Ps of MEN1—menin is lost.
