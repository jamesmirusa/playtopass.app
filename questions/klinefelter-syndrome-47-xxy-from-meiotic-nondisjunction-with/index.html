USMLE Step 1 practice question: Klinefelter syndrome: 47,XXY from meiotic nondisjunction with small firm testes and high gonadotropins

USMLE Step 1ReproductiveGenetics

A 17-year-old boy is brought to the physician because of breast enlargement for 2 years and teasing about his body at school. He has needed extra help with reading since early childhood. He is 191 cm (6 ft 3 in) tall, with an arm span 6 cm greater than his height and long legs relative to his trunk. Examination shows bilateral gynecomastia, sparse facial and axillary hair, a normal-sized penis and small, firm testes with a volume of 2 mL each. Serum studies show a low testosterone concentration and markedly elevated FSH and LH concentrations. Semen analysis shows no sperm.

Which of the following is the most likely cause of this patient's condition?

  1. Loss of one sex chromosome, producing a 45,X karyotype
  2. Mutation of the androgen receptor gene in a 46,XY male
  3. An extra Y chromosome, producing a 47,XYY karyotype
  4. Translocation of SRY onto an X chromosome in a 46,XX individual
  5. An extra X chromosome from meiotic nondisjunction, producing a 47,XXY karyotype
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: E. An extra X chromosome from meiotic nondisjunction, producing a 47,XXY karyotype

Tall stature with eunuchoid proportions, gynecomastia, sparse body hair, small firm testes, azoospermia, learning difficulties, low testosterone and high FSH and LH indicate Klinefelter syndrome, the most common chromosomal cause of male hypogonadism (about 1 in 600 male births). The 47,XXY karyotype results from nondisjunction during meiosis in either parent, roughly half of cases from each; one X is inactivated as a Barr body. Hyalinization of the seminiferous tubules destroys Sertoli cells, so inhibin B falls and FSH rises; Leydig cell dysfunction lowers testosterone, so LH rises; and increased aromatization of androgens raises the estradiol-to-testosterone ratio, causing gynecomastia. Low testosterone delays epiphyseal closure, producing long limbs. Patients have higher risks of breast cancer, osteoporosis, autoimmune disease and venous thromboembolism. Testosterone replacement improves virilization and bone density but not fertility; some men can father children through microdissection testicular sperm extraction and in vitro fertilization.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Loss of one sex chromosome, producing a 45,X karyotype — A 45,X karyotype causes Turner syndrome in a phenotypic female with short stature and streak ovaries.
  • B. Mutation of the androgen receptor gene in a 46,XY male — Androgen insensitivity in a 46,XY individual produces female external genitalia or undervirilization, not normal male genitalia with small testes.
  • C. An extra Y chromosome, producing a 47,XYY karyotype — 47,XYY males are tall but usually have normal testes, fertility and hormone levels.
  • D. Translocation of SRY onto an X chromosome in a 46,XX individual — 46,XX males with SRY translocation are usually short and have small testes, but this patient's tall stature and learning difficulties fit 47,XXY.

Key point

Recognize Klinefelter syndrome and its hormonal pattern of low testosterone with high FSH and LH

Memory hook

Tall, gynecomastia, testes the size of a fingertip, FSH sky-high: an extra X is wearing out the seminiferous tubules.
