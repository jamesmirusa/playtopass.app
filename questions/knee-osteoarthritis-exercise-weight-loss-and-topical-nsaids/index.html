USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Knee osteoarthritis: exercise, weight loss and topical NSAIDs as first-line therapy (ACR 2019)

USMLE Step 2 CKMusculoskeletalInternal Medicine

A 64-year-old woman comes to the physician because of pain in both knees for 2 years. The pain is worse when she climbs stairs and at the end of the day and is relieved by rest; morning stiffness lasts about 15 minutes. She has hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus, and her estimated glomerular filtration rate is 52 mL/min/1.73 m². She has tried oral acetaminophen with little benefit. Her BMI is 33 kg/m². Examination shows bony enlargement and crepitus of both knees, mild varus deformity, no warmth and no effusion. X-rays of the knees show medial joint space narrowing, osteophytes and subchondral sclerosis.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

  1. Daily oral tramadol
  2. Exercise, weight loss and topical diclofenac
  3. Intra-articular hyaluronic acid injections
  4. Oral glucosamine and chondroitin
  5. Referral for total knee arthroplasty
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Exercise, weight loss and topical diclofenac

Activity-related knee pain relieved by rest, brief morning stiffness, bony enlargement, crepitus and radiographic joint space narrowing with osteophytes indicate osteoarthritis. The 2019 ACR/Arthritis Foundation guideline strongly recommends exercise (strengthening, aerobic or aquatic), weight loss of at least 5% in patients with overweight or obesity, and topical NSAIDs for knee osteoarthritis. Topical NSAIDs are especially suitable here because their low systemic absorption avoids the renal, gastrointestinal and cardiovascular risks of oral NSAIDs in a patient with chronic kidney disease and hypertension. The guideline recommends against glucosamine and chondroitin for the knee and conditionally against intra-articular hyaluronic acid. Tramadol is only a conditional option when other measures fail or are contraindicated, and other opioids are discouraged. Total knee arthroplasty is considered for severe symptoms and functional limitation despite an adequate trial of nonsurgical treatment.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Daily oral tramadol — Tramadol is a conditional, later option with dependence and adverse-effect risks, not first-line therapy.
  • C. Intra-articular hyaluronic acid injections — The ACR conditionally recommends against hyaluronic acid injections for knee osteoarthritis.
  • D. Oral glucosamine and chondroitin — Glucosamine and chondroitin are not effective for knee osteoarthritis and are recommended against.
  • E. Referral for total knee arthroplasty — Arthroplasty is for severe disease after nonsurgical treatment has been adequately tried.

Key point

Start exercise, weight loss and topical NSAIDs as first-line treatment for knee osteoarthritis

Memory hook

Creaky knees and CKD: move more, weigh less, and rub the NSAID on instead of swallowing it.
