USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Painless third-trimester bleeding: ultrasonography before digital examination (placenta previa)

USMLE Step 2 CKPregnancyObstetrics & Gynecology

A 34-year-old woman, gravida 3, para 2, at 31 weeks' gestation comes to the emergency department 40 minutes after the sudden onset of vaginal bleeding that soaked two pads. She has no abdominal pain or contractions and has had no trauma or recent intercourse. Both of her children were delivered by cesarean delivery. She moved to the area 4 months ago and has not had prenatal care since 12 weeks' gestation; no second-trimester ultrasonography was performed. She does not smoke or use illicit drugs. Temperature is 36.8°C (98.2°F), pulse is 98/min, respirations are 18/min, and blood pressure is 116/72 mm Hg. The abdomen is soft, and the uterus is nontender with no palpable contractions; fundal height is 31 cm. Bright red blood is present on the perineum, and the bleeding has slowed. The fetal heart rate tracing shows a baseline of 145/min with moderate variability and accelerations. Two large-bore intravenous catheters are placed, and blood is sent for a complete blood count, coagulation studies, and type and crossmatch. Her blood type is O, Rh-positive.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

  1. Digital cervical examination to assess dilation
  2. Ultrasonography to determine placental location
  3. MRI of the pelvis to assess placental invasion
  4. Amniotomy followed by oxytocin infusion
  5. Immediate cesarean delivery
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Ultrasonography to determine placental location

Painless, bright red vaginal bleeding in the third trimester with a soft, nontender uterus and a reassuring fetal heart rate tracing is placenta previa until proven otherwise. Risk factors include previous cesarean deliveries (she has had two), multiparity, maternal age ≥35 years, smoking, multiple gestation, in vitro fertilization, and previous previa. Placental abruption, by contrast, typically causes painful bleeding with a tender, firm (hypertonic) uterus, frequent contractions, and often fetal heart rate abnormalities; its risk factors include hypertension, cocaine use, smoking, abdominal trauma, and preterm prelabor rupture of membranes.

Because she did not have a second-trimester anatomy scan, her placental location is unknown. A digital cervical examination is contraindicated until previa has been excluded, because a finger passed through the cervix can disrupt the placenta and provoke catastrophic hemorrhage. After maternal stabilization (intravenous access, laboratory studies, crossmatch, and continuous fetal monitoring), the next step is ultrasonography: transabdominal scanning followed by transvaginal ultrasonography, which is safe (the probe does not enter the cervix) and more accurate for measuring the distance between the placental edge and the internal os.

If previa is confirmed and mother and fetus remain stable, management includes hospitalization, antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone) because she is <34 weeks with bleeding and at risk of preterm delivery within 7 days, magnesium sulfate for fetal neuroprotection if delivery becomes imminent before 32 weeks, anti-D immune globulin if Rh-negative, and planned cesarean delivery at 36 0/7–37 6/7 weeks. With two previous cesarean deliveries, an anterior low-lying placenta raises concern for placenta accreta spectrum, which is evaluated by ultrasonography with MRI as an adjunct. Emergency cesarean delivery is indicated for life-threatening hemorrhage or a nonreassuring fetal status.

High-yield summary: third-trimester bleeding — painless with soft uterus = placenta previa (no digital examination until ultrasonography); painful with tender, hypertonic uterus = abruption (clinical diagnosis); painless bleeding at membrane rupture with fetal bradycardia = vasa previa.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Digital cervical examination to assess dilation — A digital cervical examination is contraindicated until placenta previa has been excluded by ultrasonography, because it can precipitate massive hemorrhage.
  • C. MRI of the pelvis to assess placental invasion — MRI is an adjunct for evaluating placenta accreta spectrum after ultrasonography has localized the placenta; it is not the initial study in acute bleeding.
  • D. Amniotomy followed by oxytocin infusion — Amniotomy and labor induction are contraindicated if placenta previa is present, because vaginal delivery is contraindicated; this stable preterm pregnancy also has no indication for delivery.
  • E. Immediate cesarean delivery — Mother and fetus are stable, bleeding is slowing, and the pregnancy is 31 weeks; immediate cesarean delivery is reserved for life-threatening hemorrhage or a nonreassuring fetal status.

Key point

In painless third-trimester vaginal bleeding, ultrasonography to locate the placenta must precede any digital cervical examination.

Memory hook

Painless bleeding late in pregnancy: 'No finger before the picture' — ultrasound the placenta before any digital exam.
