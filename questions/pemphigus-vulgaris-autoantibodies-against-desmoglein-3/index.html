USMLE Step 1 practice question: Pemphigus vulgaris: autoantibodies against desmoglein 3

USMLE Step 1DermatologyImmunology

A 52-year-old woman comes to the physician because of painful mouth sores for 2 months and blisters on her trunk for 3 weeks. She has had difficulty eating because of oral pain. Examination shows several erosions of the buccal mucosa and soft palate, and flaccid bullae and crusted erosions on the chest and back. Gentle lateral pressure on normal-appearing skin next to a lesion causes the epidermis to separate. A skin biopsy specimen shows a suprabasal split with acantholytic cells; the basal cells remain attached to the basement membrane. Direct immunofluorescence shows IgG deposited in a net-like pattern between keratinocytes throughout the epidermis.

This patient's autoantibodies are most likely directed against which of the following?

  1. Bullous pemphigoid antigen 180 (type XVII collagen)
  2. Desmoglein 3
  3. Type VII collagen
  4. Epidermal transglutaminase
  5. Laminin-332 of the lamina lucida
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Desmoglein 3

Painful oral erosions followed by flaccid, easily ruptured bullae, a positive Nikolsky sign, suprabasal acantholysis and intercellular IgG in a fishnet pattern indicate pemphigus vulgaris. It is a type II hypersensitivity reaction: IgG autoantibodies bind desmoglein 3 (and often desmoglein 1), cadherin proteins of the desmosomes that join keratinocytes. Loss of cell-to-cell adhesion makes keratinocytes round up and separate (acantholysis) just above the basal layer, while basal cells stay attached to the basement membrane, giving a row of 'tombstones' on biopsy. Because the split is within the epidermis, the roof of the blister is thin and the bullae are flaccid. Treatment is systemic glucocorticoids with rituximab; untreated disease can be fatal.

In bullous pemphigoid, antibodies target hemidesmosome proteins (BP180 and BP230) at the dermal-epidermal junction. The split is subepidermal, the bullae are tense, the mucosa is usually spared, and immunofluorescence shows linear deposits along the basement membrane.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Bullous pemphigoid antigen 180 (type XVII collagen) — BP180 is a hemidesmosome antigen in bullous pemphigoid, which causes tense subepidermal bullae, usually without oral involvement, and linear basement membrane staining.
  • C. Type VII collagen — Type VII collagen of the anchoring fibrils is the target in epidermolysis bullosa acquisita, a subepidermal blistering disease of skin exposed to trauma.
  • D. Epidermal transglutaminase — Epidermal transglutaminase is targeted in dermatitis herpetiformis, which causes intensely pruritic grouped vesicles on extensor surfaces with granular IgA in the dermal papillae.
  • E. Laminin-332 of the lamina lucida — Laminin-332 is targeted in some forms of mucous membrane pemphigoid, a subepidermal disease with linear basement membrane deposits and scarring.

Key point

Pemphigus vulgaris is caused by IgG against desmoglein 3 (and 1): suprabasal acantholysis, flaccid bullae, oral erosions, Nikolsky sign, intercellular net-like immunofluorescence.

Memory hook

Pemphigus: desmosome breaks, superficial flaccid blister, the net glows. Pemphigoid: hemidesmosome breaks, deep tense blister, the line glows.
