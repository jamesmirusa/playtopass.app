USMLE Step 1 practice question: Myasthenia gravis: autoantibodies against postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction

USMLE Step 1NervousImmunology

A 32-year-old woman comes to the physician because of drooping eyelids and double vision for 2 months. Her symptoms are mild in the morning and worsen in the evening and after reading. She also has difficulty chewing at the end of meals. Examination shows bilateral fluctuating ptosis that worsens with sustained upward gaze and improves after an ice pack is applied for 2 minutes. Deep tendon reflexes and sensation are normal. Repetitive nerve stimulation shows a decremental response. CT of the chest shows an anterior mediastinal mass.

Antibodies against which of the following are most likely responsible for this patient's condition?

  1. Presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels
  2. Voltage-gated sodium channels of the axon
  3. Muscarinic acetylcholine receptors
  4. Postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors
  5. Acetylcholinesterase
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors

Fatigable weakness of ocular and bulbar muscles that worsens with use and through the day, improves with rest or cooling (the ice-pack test), with normal reflexes and sensation and a decremental response to repetitive stimulation, is myasthenia gravis. Most patients have IgG autoantibodies against the postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptor at the neuromuscular junction (some have antibodies against MuSK). The antibodies block the receptor, increase its internalization and degradation and activate complement that damages the postsynaptic membrane, reducing the endplate potential so that fewer muscle fibers reach threshold with repeated firing. It is a type II hypersensitivity, and thymic hyperplasia or thymoma, as suggested by the anterior mediastinal mass, is common. Treatment includes acetylcholinesterase inhibitors such as pyridostigmine, immunosuppression and thymectomy. Antibodies against presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels cause Lambert-Eaton syndrome, in which strength improves with repeated use and reflexes are reduced.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels — Presynaptic calcium channel antibodies cause Lambert-Eaton syndrome, with weakness improving on use.
  • B. Voltage-gated sodium channels of the axon — Antibodies against axonal sodium channels do not cause fatigable ocular weakness.
  • C. Muscarinic acetylcholine receptors — Skeletal muscle uses nicotinic, not muscarinic, receptors at the neuromuscular junction.
  • E. Acetylcholinesterase — Acetylcholinesterase is the target of the treatment, not of the autoantibodies.

Key point

Identify antibodies against postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors as the cause of myasthenia gravis

Memory hook

Ptosis worse by evening, better with ice, decrement on stimulation, thymoma: antibodies block nicotinic ACh receptors.
