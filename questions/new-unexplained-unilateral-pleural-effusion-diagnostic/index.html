USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: New unexplained unilateral pleural effusion: diagnostic thoracentesis with Light's criteria and cytology

USMLE Step 2 CKRespiratoryInternal Medicine

A 67-year-old man comes to the physician because of increasing shortness of breath on exertion for 2 months and a 5-kg (11-lb) weight loss. He smoked one pack of cigarettes daily for 40 years and quit 3 years ago. He has no history of heart, liver or kidney disease and takes no medications. His temperature is 37.1°C (98.8°F), pulse is 92/min, and blood pressure is 132/80 mm Hg. Oxygen saturation is 94% on room air. There is dullness to percussion and decreased breath sounds over the lower half of the right hemithorax. There is no jugular venous distention or leg edema. A chest x-ray shows a moderate right pleural effusion without mediastinal shift. Serum albumin concentration is 3.6 g/dL, and serum creatinine concentration is normal.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

  1. A trial of oral furosemide
  2. Chest tube placement with talc pleurodesis
  3. Repeat chest x-ray in 6 weeks
  4. Diagnostic thoracentesis
  5. Bronchoscopy before any pleural procedure
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Diagnostic thoracentesis

A new unilateral pleural effusion without an obvious cause such as heart failure should be sampled. Diagnostic thoracentesis (ideally ultrasound-guided) allows classification by Light's criteria: an exudate has a pleural fluid to serum protein ratio above 0.5, a pleural fluid to serum LDH ratio above 0.6, or pleural fluid LDH above two thirds of the upper limit of normal serum LDH. Fluid is also sent for cell count, glucose, pH, Gram stain and culture, and cytology; in a former heavy smoker with weight loss, malignancy (lung cancer, mesothelioma, metastases) is a leading concern, and a negative first cytology may need repeat sampling or thoracoscopic pleural biopsy. Diuretics treat transudates from heart failure, which he lacks. Pleurodesis is considered only for recurrent symptomatic malignant effusions once the diagnosis is made, and watchful waiting delays diagnosis.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. A trial of oral furosemide — He has no signs of heart failure; a diuretic trial would delay diagnosis of a likely exudate.
  • B. Chest tube placement with talc pleurodesis — Pleurodesis is for recurrent symptomatic malignant effusion after the diagnosis is established.
  • C. Repeat chest x-ray in 6 weeks — Waiting delays the diagnosis of a possibly malignant effusion.
  • E. Bronchoscopy before any pleural procedure — Pleural fluid analysis is the first and least invasive test; bronchoscopy may follow if needed.

Key point

Evaluate a new unexplained pleural effusion with diagnostic thoracentesis and Light's criteria

Memory hook

New one-sided effusion, smoker, weight loss: tap it—Light's criteria plus cytology.
