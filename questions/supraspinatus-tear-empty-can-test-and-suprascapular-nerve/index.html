USMLE Step 1 practice question: Supraspinatus tear: empty-can test and suprascapular nerve

USMLE Step 1MusculoskeletalAnatomy

A 58-year-old house painter comes to the physician because of pain in his right shoulder for 3 months. The pain is worse when he works with his arms overhead and wakes him at night when he lies on that side. One week ago, the pain worsened suddenly when he lifted a heavy paint bucket onto a shelf. Examination shows tenderness over the greater tubercle of the humerus. He has difficulty initiating abduction of the right arm, but passive range of motion is full. With both arms abducted to 90 degrees in the plane of the scapula, elbows extended and thumbs pointing toward the floor, he has pain and marked weakness on the right when the examiner presses the arms downward. External rotation strength with the arm at his side is normal. MRI of the shoulder shows a full-thickness tear of one rotator cuff tendon.

The injured muscle is innervated by which of the following nerves?

  1. Axillary nerve
  2. Suprascapular nerve
  3. Upper subscapular nerve
  4. Long thoracic nerve
  5. Thoracodorsal nerve
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Suprascapular nerve

Pain with overhead activity, night pain when lying on the affected side, tenderness over the greater tubercle, difficulty initiating abduction with preserved passive motion, and pain and weakness on the empty-can (Jobe) test indicate a tear of the supraspinatus tendon. Normal external rotation strength argues against involvement of the infraspinatus or teres minor.

The supraspinatus arises from the supraspinous fossa of the scapula, passes beneath the acromion and coracoacromial ligament, and inserts on the superior facet of the greater tubercle. It initiates abduction (approximately the first 15 degrees), after which the deltoid (axillary nerve) takes over; it also helps hold the humeral head in the glenoid. It is innervated by the suprascapular nerve (C5–C6), which arises from the superior trunk of the brachial plexus and passes through the suprascapular notch beneath the superior transverse scapular ligament to supply the supraspinatus, then continues around the spinoglenoid notch to supply the infraspinatus.

The supraspinatus is the most commonly torn rotator cuff tendon because it is compressed between the humeral head and the coracoacromial arch during overhead movements (impingement), and degenerative tears become more frequent with age. In the drop arm test, a patient with a large tear cannot slowly lower the arm from full abduction. Of the other rotator cuff muscles, the infraspinatus (suprascapular nerve) and teres minor (axillary nerve) externally rotate the arm, and the subscapularis (upper and lower subscapular nerves) internally rotates it.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Axillary nerve — The axillary nerve supplies the deltoid and teres minor; injury impairs abduction beyond the first 15 degrees and causes numbness over the lateral shoulder.
  • C. Upper subscapular nerve — The upper subscapular nerve supplies the subscapularis, an internal rotator assessed with the lift-off or belly-press test.
  • D. Long thoracic nerve — The long thoracic nerve supplies the serratus anterior; injury causes winging of the scapula, not weakness on the empty-can test.
  • E. Thoracodorsal nerve — The thoracodorsal nerve supplies the latissimus dorsi, which extends, adducts and internally rotates the arm.

Key point

The supraspinatus initiates arm abduction, is tested with the empty-can test, is the most commonly torn rotator cuff tendon and is innervated by the suprascapular nerve.

Memory hook

Supraspinatus starts the swing (first 15 degrees), the deltoid finishes it. Empty the can to test; the suprascapular nerve powers it.
