USMLE Step 1 practice question: Barrett esophagus: intestinal metaplasia as a reversible adaptive change that predisposes to dysplasia

USMLE Step 1General PrinciplesPathology

A 57-year-old man with obesity comes to the physician because of heartburn several times a week for 15 years, for which he takes over-the-counter antacids. He has no dysphagia, weight loss or vomiting. Upper endoscopy shows a tongue of salmon-pink mucosa extending 4 cm above the gastroesophageal junction. Biopsy specimens from this area show that the normal stratified squamous epithelium has been replaced by columnar epithelium containing scattered goblet cells. The cells have basal nuclei with normal size and shape, and there is no loss of polarity, increased mitoses or invasion.

Which of the following best describes the change in this patient's esophageal epithelium?

  1. Hyperplasia
  2. Hypertrophy
  3. Dysplasia
  4. Metaplasia
  5. Anaplasia
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Metaplasia

Barrett esophagus is the replacement of the distal esophageal squamous epithelium by columnar epithelium with goblet cells (intestinal metaplasia) in response to chronic acid and bile reflux. Metaplasia is a reversible change in which one adult cell type is replaced by another type better able to withstand the stress; it arises from reprogramming of stem or progenitor cells, which switch their differentiation program (for example under the transcription factor CDX2), rather than from transformation of mature cells. The new epithelium resists acid but loses the protective features of squamous mucosa and is genetically unstable: metaplasia can progress to low- and high-grade dysplasia and to esophageal adenocarcinoma. The biopsy here shows normal nuclei and preserved polarity, so there is no dysplasia, which would show nuclear enlargement, pleomorphism, hyperchromasia and loss of orientation. Hyperplasia is an increase in cell number, hypertrophy an increase in cell size, and anaplasia the lack of differentiation seen in malignant tumors.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Hyperplasia — Hyperplasia is an increase in the number of cells of the same type, not a change to a new cell type.
  • B. Hypertrophy — Hypertrophy is an increase in cell size without a change in cell type.
  • C. Dysplasia — Dysplasia means disordered growth with atypical nuclei and loss of polarity, which this biopsy lacks.
  • E. Anaplasia — Anaplasia is the lack of differentiation typical of malignant cells.

Key point

Classify Barrett esophagus as intestinal metaplasia that can progress to dysplasia and adenocarcinoma

Memory hook

Squamous swapped for goblet-cell columnar: Barrett is metaplasia—reversible, but the first step toward adenocarcinoma.
