USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Simple febrile seizure in a well-appearing toddler: reassurance without lumbar puncture, EEG or imaging

USMLE Step 2 CKPediatricsPediatrics

An 18-month-old boy is brought to the emergency department 30 minutes after a seizure at home. His mother says he had a runny nose and fever since yesterday; this afternoon his whole body stiffened and jerked for about 2 minutes, and he was sleepy for 15 minutes afterward. He has never had a seizure before. His development is normal, and his vaccinations are up to date. His temperature is 39.4°C (102.9°F). He is now alert, playful and drinking. There is clear nasal discharge. The fontanelle is closed, the neck is supple, and neurologic examination is normal.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

  1. Lumbar puncture
  2. Reassurance and fever control for comfort
  3. Electroencephalography
  4. CT of the head
  5. Daily levetiracetam
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Reassurance and fever control for comfort

A generalized seizure lasting less than 15 minutes, occurring once in 24 hours, in a neurologically normal child aged 6 months to 5 years with fever and no CNS infection, is a simple febrile seizure. The AAP advises against routine EEG, neuroimaging and blood tests, and lumbar puncture is needed only with meningeal signs or symptoms, or considered in infants 6 to 12 months old who are unvaccinated against Haemophilus influenzae type b or pneumococcus, or pretreated with antibiotics. The evaluation focuses on the source of fever. Parents should be reassured: about one third of children have a recurrence, but the risk of later epilepsy is only slightly higher than in the general population, and there is no harm to development. Antipyretics make the child more comfortable but do not prevent recurrences, and continuous or intermittent antiseizure drugs are not recommended because their adverse effects outweigh the small benefits.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Lumbar puncture — Lumbar puncture is reserved for meningeal signs or selected unvaccinated or antibiotic-pretreated infants.
  • C. Electroencephalography — EEG is not indicated after a simple febrile seizure and does not predict recurrence or epilepsy.
  • D. CT of the head — Neuroimaging is not indicated in a neurologically normal child after a simple febrile seizure.
  • E. Daily levetiracetam — Antiseizure drugs are not recommended; their adverse effects outweigh the small benefit.

Key point

Manage a simple febrile seizure with reassurance and evaluation of the fever source only

Memory hook

Two-minute fever fit, toddler now playing: simple febrile seizure—no LP, EEG or CT, just reassure.
