USMLE Step 1 practice question: Ureteral injury during uterine artery ligation at hysterectomy

USMLE Step 1ReproductiveAnatomy

A 46-year-old woman undergoes total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingectomy for symptomatic uterine leiomyomas; both ovaries are preserved. During the procedure, brisk bleeding near the right lateral aspect of the cervix is controlled with several deep sutures. On postoperative day 3, she has right flank pain and a temperature of 38.3°C (100.9°F). Serum creatinine concentration has increased from 0.8 mg/dL preoperatively to 1.3 mg/dL. CT urography shows right hydroureteronephrosis with abrupt narrowing of the distal right ureter about 2 cm lateral to the cervix, just above the lateral vaginal fornix; there is no contrast extravasation. The left ureter is normal.

The ureteral injury most likely occurred during ligation of which of the following structures?

  1. Ovarian vessels within the suspensory ligament of the ovary
  2. Round ligament of the uterus near the deep inguinal ring
  3. Ovarian ligament joining the ovary to the uterine horn
  4. Obturator neurovascular bundle on the lateral pelvic wall
  5. Uterine artery beside the cervix in the cardinal ligament
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: E. Uterine artery beside the cervix in the cardinal ligament

Flank pain, fever and a rise in creatinine after hysterectomy, with unilateral hydroureteronephrosis and abrupt narrowing of the distal ureter just lateral to the cervix, indicate that the ureter has been ligated or kinked by a suture. The location and the difficult hemostasis beside the cervix point to injury during ligation of the uterine artery.

The ureter enters the pelvis by crossing the pelvic brim anterior to the bifurcation of the common iliac artery, runs along the lateral pelvic wall anterior to the internal iliac artery and behind the ovary, and then turns medially at the level of the ischial spine to pass through the base of the broad ligament (the cardinal, or transverse cervical, ligament). Here, about 1.5 to 2 cm lateral to the cervix and just above the lateral vaginal fornix, it passes beneath the uterine artery ('water under the bridge') before entering the bladder. Clamping or suturing the uterine artery, or deep suturing to control bleeding in this region, can catch, kink or devascularize the ureter.

The other classic site of iatrogenic ureteral injury is the pelvic brim, where the ureter lies just medial to the ovarian vessels in the suspensory (infundibulopelvic) ligament; it is at risk when that ligament is clamped during oophorectomy. Ureteral injury may also occur near the uterosacral ligaments and during closure of the vaginal cuff. Unrecognized ligation causes obstruction with flank pain and hydronephrosis, whereas transection or devascularization can cause a urinoma or a ureterovaginal fistula with continuous vaginal leakage of urine.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Ovarian vessels within the suspensory ligament of the ovary — The ureter is at risk at the pelvic brim when the suspensory ligament is clamped, but the ovaries were preserved and the narrowing lies beside the cervix.
  • B. Round ligament of the uterus near the deep inguinal ring — The round ligament runs from the uterine horn through the deep inguinal ring, anterior to and far from the course of the ureter.
  • C. Ovarian ligament joining the ovary to the uterine horn — The ovarian ligament is divided when the ovaries are preserved, but it lies high near the uterine horn, well away from the ureter.
  • D. Obturator neurovascular bundle on the lateral pelvic wall — The obturator nerve and vessels are at risk during pelvic lymph node dissection, which was not performed, and do not lie beside the distal ureter at the cervix.

Key point

The distal ureter passes beneath the uterine artery about 1.5 to 2 cm lateral to the cervix in the cardinal ligament, making it vulnerable to ligation during hysterectomy.

Memory hook

Water under the bridge: the ureter (water) runs under the uterine artery (bridge) beside the cervix. Tie the bridge carefully.
