USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Severe Plasmodium falciparum malaria: intravenous artesunate

USMLE Step 2 CKMicrobiologyInternal Medicine

A 34-year-old man is brought to the emergency department because of 4 days of high fevers with rigors, headache and vomiting, and confusion since this morning. He returned 10 days ago from a 3-week visit to relatives in Nigeria and did not take malaria prophylaxis. His temperature is 40.1°C (104.2°F), pulse is 124/min, and blood pressure is 98/60 mm Hg. He is drowsy and disoriented, with mild scleral icterus and no neck stiffness. Laboratory studies show a hemoglobin concentration of 8.1 g/dL, platelet count of 42,000/mm³, glucose concentration of 64 mg/dL, creatinine concentration of 2.4 mg/dL and total bilirubin concentration of 4.2 mg/dL. A thin blood smear shows ring forms with multiple parasites in some red blood cells and banana-shaped gametocytes; parasitemia is 9%.

Which of the following is the most appropriate antimalarial treatment?

  1. Oral chloroquine
  2. Oral atovaquone-proguanil
  3. Oral artemether-lumefantrine
  4. Oral primaquine
  5. Intravenous artesunate
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: E. Intravenous artesunate

Fever after travel to sub-Saharan Africa without prophylaxis, ring forms with multiple infected red cells, banana-shaped gametocytes and high parasitemia indicate Plasmodium falciparum malaria. Impaired consciousness, parasitemia of 5% or more, severe anemia, acute kidney injury, jaundice and hypoglycemia each define severe malaria, which can progress rapidly to death. Infected red cells express PfEMP1 and adhere to endothelium, obstructing the microcirculation of the brain, kidneys and other organs. Intravenous artesunate is the treatment of choice for severe malaria in the United States (it replaced intravenous quinidine) because it clears parasites faster and lowers mortality compared with quinine. After at least 24 hours of intravenous therapy and once the patient can take oral medication, a full course of an oral regimen such as artemether-lumefantrine or atovaquone-proguanil is given. Supportive care includes monitoring of glucose, fluids, treatment of seizures and dialysis if needed; delayed hemolysis can occur after artesunate, so hemoglobin is checked for several weeks.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Oral chloroquine — Most P falciparum, including strains from West Africa, is resistant to chloroquine, and oral therapy is inadequate for severe disease.
  • B. Oral atovaquone-proguanil — Atovaquone-proguanil is used for uncomplicated malaria or to complete treatment after intravenous artesunate.
  • C. Oral artemether-lumefantrine — Artemether-lumefantrine treats uncomplicated malaria and completes therapy after the intravenous phase, but oral drugs alone are inadequate in severe malaria.
  • D. Oral primaquine — Primaquine eradicates liver hypnozoites of P vivax and P ovale and does not treat severe falciparum blood-stage infection.

Key point

Treat severe falciparum malaria with intravenous artesunate

Memory hook

Back from Nigeria, confused, 9% parasitemia: severe falciparum—artesunate goes in the vein now.
