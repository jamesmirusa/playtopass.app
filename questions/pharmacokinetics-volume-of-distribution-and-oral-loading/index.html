USMLE Step 1 practice question: Pharmacokinetics: volume of distribution and oral loading dose

USMLE Step 1PharmacologyPharmacology

A 58-year-old woman with recurrent ventricular tachycardia is enrolled in a clinical trial of an investigational oral antiarrhythmic drug. During a pharmacokinetic study, she receives a single 400-mg intravenous bolus of the drug. The plasma concentration extrapolated to time zero is 8 mg/L, and 6 hours later the plasma concentration is 4 mg/L. The drug follows first-order, one-compartment kinetics, and its oral bioavailability is 60%. Her serum creatinine concentration and liver function tests are within the reference ranges. The investigators plan to rapidly achieve a target plasma concentration of 6 mg/L with a single oral dose.

Which of the following is the most appropriate oral loading dose?

  1. 180 mg
  2. 300 mg
  3. 500 mg
  4. 833 mg
  5. 1000 mg
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 500 mg

A loading dose is intended to reach the target plasma concentration immediately by “filling” the volume of distribution (Vd); it depends on Vd and bioavailability (F) but not on clearance or half-life: Loading dose = (target Cp × Vd) / F.

Step 1 – volume of distribution: Vd = dose / C₀ = 400 mg / 8 mg/L = 50 L. The concentration extrapolated to time zero must be used because it reflects distribution of the entire dose before any elimination. Step 2 – loading dose: (6 mg/L × 50 L) / 0.6 = 300 mg / 0.6 = 500 mg.

The 6-hour concentration gives the half-life (the concentration halves from 8 to 4 mg/L in 6 hours, so t½ = 6 hours) and allows clearance to be calculated (CL = 0.693 × Vd / t½ = 0.693 × 50 L / 6 h ≈ 5.8 L/h), but these values determine the maintenance dose (maintenance dose rate = Cp,ss × CL / F), not the loading dose. Without a loading dose, about 4–5 half-lives (24–30 hours here) would be required to approach steady state. In renal or hepatic impairment, the loading dose is generally unchanged because Vd is unchanged, whereas the maintenance dose must be reduced.

High-yield summary: Vd = dose / C₀; loading dose = Cp × Vd / F; maintenance dose rate = Cp × CL / F; t½ = 0.693 × Vd / CL; steady state is reached in 4–5 half-lives.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 180 mg — This results from multiplying by the bioavailability instead of dividing (6 × 50 × 0.6); because only 60% of an oral dose reaches the systemic circulation, the oral dose must be larger, not smaller, than the IV dose.
  • B. 300 mg — This is the appropriate intravenous loading dose (6 mg/L × 50 L); it does not account for the 60% oral bioavailability.
  • D. 833 mg — This results from correcting for bioavailability twice (300 mg ÷ 0.6 ÷ 0.6).
  • E. 1000 mg — This results from calculating Vd with the 6-hour concentration (400 mg ÷ 4 mg/L = 100 L); after elimination has begun, the measured concentration underestimates the initial concentration and overestimates Vd.

Key point

Loading dose = (target plasma concentration × Vd) / bioavailability; Vd is calculated from the IV dose and the concentration extrapolated to time zero, and the loading dose does not depend on clearance or half-life.

Memory hook

Loading dose fills the tank: LD = Cp × Vd / F. Tank size (Vd) matters; the leak (clearance, half-life) matters only for maintenance.
