USMLE Step 1 practice question: Plaque psoriasis: parakeratosis, Munro microabscesses and the IL-23/Th17 axis

USMLE Step 1DermatologyPathology

A 34-year-old woman comes to the physician because of a 6-month history of itchy, scaly skin lesions on her elbows, knees and scalp. The lesions worsened after a streptococcal sore throat and tend to appear in scratch marks. Examination shows well-demarcated, salmon-pink plaques covered by thick silvery scale; removing a scale produces pinpoint bleeding. Several fingernails show pitting and yellow-brown discoloration. A biopsy specimen of a plaque shows a thickened epidermis with elongated rete ridges, retained nuclei in the stratum corneum, an absent granular layer, and collections of neutrophils within the stratum corneum.

Which of the following cytokines is the most important driver of the keratinocyte proliferation in this patient's lesions?

  1. Interleukin-4
  2. Interleukin-17
  3. Interleukin-5
  4. Interferon-α
  5. Interleukin-10
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Interleukin-17

Sharply demarcated plaques with silvery scale on extensor surfaces and the scalp, the Auspitz sign (pinpoint bleeding from dilated capillaries in thinned suprapapillary plates), the Koebner phenomenon and nail pitting are typical of plaque psoriasis. Histology shows acanthosis with regular elongation of rete ridges, parakeratosis (nuclei retained in the stratum corneum because keratinocyte transit is accelerated), loss of the stratum granulosum, and Munro microabscesses (neutrophils in the stratum corneum). Psoriasis is driven by dermal dendritic cells that secrete interleukin-23, which maintains Th17 cells; their interleukin-17 and interleukin-22, together with tumor necrosis factor-α, stimulate keratinocyte proliferation and neutrophil recruitment. This explains the efficacy of antibodies against IL-17A (secukinumab, ixekizumab), IL-23 (guselkumab, risankizumab), IL-12/23 (ustekinumab) and TNF-α.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Interleukin-4 — Interleukin-4 drives Th2 responses and IgE class switching and is central to atopic dermatitis (target of dupilumab).
  • C. Interleukin-5 — Interleukin-5 promotes eosinophil differentiation and survival and matters in eosinophilic asthma, not psoriasis.
  • D. Interferon-α — Interferon-α contributes to cutaneous lupus and can trigger psoriasis flares, but it is not the main effector cytokine of plaques.
  • E. Interleukin-10 — Interleukin-10 is anti-inflammatory and is relatively deficient in psoriatic skin.

Key point

Correlate the histologic features of psoriasis with the IL-23/Th17/IL-17 pathway

Memory hook

Silvery scale, nuclei in the horny layer, neutrophil nests on top: psoriasis runs on IL-23 → Th17 → IL-17.
