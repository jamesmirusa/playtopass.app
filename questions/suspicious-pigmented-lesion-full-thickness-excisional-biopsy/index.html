USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Suspicious pigmented lesion: full-thickness excisional biopsy with narrow margins

USMLE Step 2 CKDermatologySurgery

A 52-year-old man comes to the physician because his wife noticed that a mole on his upper back has grown and darkened over the past 6 months. He worked as a roofer for 20 years and had several blistering sunburns in childhood. Examination shows a 9-mm, asymmetric, flat lesion with an irregular, notched border and variegated brown, black and blue-gray color on the left upper back. It is not ulcerated. No other atypical lesions are seen, and there is no cervical or axillary lymphadenopathy.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

  1. Superficial shave biopsy of the darkest part of the lesion
  2. Wide local excision with 2-cm margins
  3. Sentinel lymph node biopsy
  4. Photography of the lesion and reexamination in 3 months
  5. Full-thickness excisional biopsy with 1- to 3-mm margins
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: E. Full-thickness excisional biopsy with 1- to 3-mm margins

Asymmetry, border irregularity, color variegation, diameter greater than 6 mm and evolution (the ABCDE criteria) make this lesion suspicious for melanoma. The most important prognostic factor in localized melanoma is the Breslow depth, measured from the granular layer to the deepest tumor cell, and it determines the margin of definitive excision and whether sentinel lymph node biopsy is needed. The initial procedure must therefore remove the full thickness of the lesion: an excisional biopsy (elliptical, punch or deep saucerization) with narrow 1- to 3-mm margins of normal skin extending into subcutaneous fat. Wide local excision follows once the depth is known: 0.5 to 1 cm for in situ disease, 1 cm for tumors up to 1 mm thick, 1 to 2 cm for 1 to 2 mm, and 2 cm for thicker tumors. Sentinel node biopsy is generally offered for tumors thicker than about 1 mm or with ulceration.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Superficial shave biopsy of the darkest part of the lesion — A superficial shave may transect the tumor base and make accurate measurement of depth impossible.
  • B. Wide local excision with 2-cm margins — Wide excision before histologic diagnosis and depth measurement may be excessive or inadequate and can disrupt lymphatic mapping.
  • C. Sentinel lymph node biopsy — Sentinel node biopsy is planned after the diagnosis and Breslow depth are established.
  • D. Photography of the lesion and reexamination in 3 months — Delaying biopsy of a changing lesion with several warning signs risks deeper invasion and metastasis.

Key point

Perform narrow-margin full-thickness excisional biopsy for a lesion suspicious for melanoma

Memory hook

ABCDE mole: take all of it and all of its depth first; the wide margins come after the pathology report.
