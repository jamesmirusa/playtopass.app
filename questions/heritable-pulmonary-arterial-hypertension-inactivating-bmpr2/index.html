USMLE Step 1 practice question: Heritable pulmonary arterial hypertension: inactivating BMPR2 mutations causing vascular smooth muscle proliferation

USMLE Step 1RespiratoryGenetics

A 29-year-old woman comes to the physician because of progressive shortness of breath on exertion for 1 year and two episodes of fainting while climbing stairs. She does not smoke, use illicit drugs or take appetite suppressants. Her mother died of a 'heart and lung problem' at age 36. Examination shows jugular venous distention, a left parasternal heave and a loud pulmonic component of the second heart sound. Pulmonary function tests and a ventilation-perfusion scan are normal. Serologic testing for HIV and connective tissue diseases is negative. Right heart catheterization shows a mean pulmonary artery pressure of 46 mm Hg, a pulmonary capillary wedge pressure of 9 mm Hg and markedly increased pulmonary vascular resistance.

An inactivating mutation in which of the following genes is most likely responsible for this patient's condition?

  1. CFTR
  2. SERPINA1
  3. BMPR2
  4. FBN1
  5. NOTCH3
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. BMPR2

Precapillary pulmonary hypertension (high mean pulmonary artery pressure with a normal wedge pressure and high pulmonary vascular resistance) in a young woman, with lung disease, chronic thromboembolism, drugs, HIV and connective tissue disease excluded and a mother who died young of a similar illness, indicates heritable pulmonary arterial hypertension. Most familial cases are caused by inactivating mutations of BMPR2, which encodes the bone morphogenetic protein receptor type 2, a member of the TGF-β receptor superfamily; inheritance is autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance, so many carriers never develop disease. BMPR2 signaling normally restrains proliferation of pulmonary vascular smooth muscle and endothelial cells; its loss leads to medial hypertrophy, intimal fibrosis and plexiform lesions that narrow small pulmonary arteries. Rising afterload causes right ventricular hypertrophy and eventually right heart failure (cor pulmonale), and exertional syncope reflects a fixed cardiac output. Treatment targets endothelin (bosentan, ambrisentan), nitric oxide–cGMP (sildenafil, riociguat) and prostacyclin (epoprostenol) pathways. CFTR causes cystic fibrosis, SERPINA1 causes α1-antitrypsin deficiency, FBN1 causes Marfan syndrome and NOTCH3 causes CADASIL.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. CFTR — CFTR mutations cause cystic fibrosis, with bronchiectasis and abnormal lung function.
  • B. SERPINA1 — SERPINA1 mutations cause α1-antitrypsin deficiency with panacinar emphysema and obstruction.
  • D. FBN1 — FBN1 mutations cause Marfan syndrome with aortic root dilation and lens dislocation.
  • E. NOTCH3 — NOTCH3 mutations cause CADASIL with subcortical strokes and dementia.

Key point

Recognize heritable pulmonary arterial hypertension and the role of BMPR2 loss in vascular remodeling

Memory hook

Young woman faints on stairs, loud P2, mother died young: BMPR2 lost—arterioles thicken, the right heart fails.
