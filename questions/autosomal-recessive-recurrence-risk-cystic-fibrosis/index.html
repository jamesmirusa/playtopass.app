USMLE Step 1 practice question: Autosomal recessive recurrence risk (cystic fibrosis, unaffected sibling = 2/3 carrier)

USMLE Step 1GeneticsGenetics

A 28-year-old woman and her 30-year-old husband come to the physician for preconception counseling. The woman's 24-year-old brother has had recurrent Pseudomonas aeruginosa pneumonia, bronchiectasis, and greasy, foul-smelling stools since childhood; he was diagnosed on the basis of an elevated sweat chloride concentration and requires pancreatic enzyme replacement. The woman, her sister, and both of her parents have no symptoms of this condition. Her husband is healthy and has no family history of this condition; the carrier frequency for this disorder in his ancestral population is 1 in 25. Neither partner has undergone genetic testing.

Before any genetic testing, which of the following is the probability that the couple's first child will be affected by the same condition as the woman's brother?

  1. 1/6
  2. 1/100
  3. 1/150
  4. 1/200
  5. 1/2500
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 1/150

The brother has cystic fibrosis (CF): recurrent Pseudomonas lung infections, bronchiectasis, pancreatic insufficiency with steatorrhea, and an elevated sweat chloride concentration. CF is autosomal recessive and is caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in CFTR, which encodes an ATP-gated chloride channel; the most common variant, F508del (ΔF508), causes protein misfolding and degradation.

Because the unaffected parents have an affected child, both must be carriers (Aa × Aa). Their offspring have a 1/4 chance of AA, 1/2 chance of Aa, and 1/4 chance of aa. The woman is known to be unaffected, so she cannot be aa; her probability of being a carrier is calculated among the three remaining outcomes: (2/4) ÷ (3/4) = 2/3. A common error is to use 1/2, which ignores the information that she is unaffected.

Probability that the child is affected = P(woman is a carrier) × P(husband is a carrier) × P(two carriers have an affected child) = 2/3 × 1/25 × 1/4 = 2/300 = 1/150.

Carrier screening of the husband would refine this risk considerably: if he tests negative, the residual risk becomes very low (screening panels detect most, but not all, pathogenic CFTR variants); if he tests positive, the risk rises to 2/3 × 1/4 = 1/6 (or 1/4 if the woman is also confirmed to be a carrier).

High-yield summary: An unaffected sibling of a patient with an autosomal recessive disease has a 2/3 carrier probability. Risk for a child of two potential carriers = P(carrier 1) × P(carrier 2) × 1/4. With a population carrier frequency of 1/25, disease incidence ≈ (1/25)² × 1/4 = 1/2500.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 1/6 — This assumes that the husband is a known carrier (2/3 × 1 × 1/4); his carrier probability is only 1/25.
  • B. 1/100 — This assumes that the woman is an obligate carrier (1 × 1/25 × 1/4); as an unaffected sibling of an affected individual, her carrier probability is 2/3, not 1.
  • D. 1/200 — This uses a carrier probability of 1/2 for the woman (1/2 × 1/25 × 1/4), ignoring that her unaffected status excludes the homozygous affected genotype; her probability is 2/3.
  • E. 1/2500 — This approximates the birth incidence of CF in the general population (1/25 × 1/25 × 1/4), which underestimates the risk for someone with an affected sibling.

Key point

An unaffected sibling of a person with an autosomal recessive disorder has a 2/3 probability of being a carrier; the risk to offspring equals the product of each parent's carrier probability and 1/4.

Memory hook

Healthy sib, recessive disease: strike 'aa' from the Punnett square → 2 of 3 boxes carry. Risk = 2/3 × 1/25 × 1/4 = 1/150.
