USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Erectile dysfunction in a man taking long-acting nitrates: PDE-5 inhibitors contraindicated, vacuum erection device instead

USMLE Step 2 CKReproductiveInternal Medicine

A 64-year-old man comes to the physician because of difficulty maintaining an erection for the past year. He has coronary artery disease with stable angina, treated with isosorbide mononitrate 60 mg daily, metoprolol, atorvastatin and aspirin, and he uses sublingual nitroglycerin about once a month. He walks 2 miles a day without chest pain. His wife is supportive. His blood pressure is 128/76 mm Hg. Genital examination is normal. Morning serum testosterone concentration is 520 ng/dL (N 300–1000). His cardiologist does not want to stop the long-acting nitrate.

Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

  1. Sildenafil 25 mg as needed
  2. Daily tadalafil 5 mg
  3. Testosterone replacement therapy
  4. Vacuum erection device
  5. Sildenafil taken 24 hours after the last nitrate dose
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Vacuum erection device

PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) block breakdown of cGMP; nitrates increase cGMP by releasing nitric oxide, so the combination causes severe, sometimes fatal, hypotension. PDE-5 inhibitors are therefore contraindicated in any man taking nitrates regularly or intermittently, including daily long-acting nitrates, for which no safe dosing window exists; the 24-hour (sildenafil, vardenafil) or 48-hour (tadalafil) intervals apply only to men who rarely use nitroglycerin and have stopped it. The AUA guideline lists vacuum erection devices, intraurethral or intracavernosal alprostadil and penile prosthesis as effective options that do not interact with nitrates; a vacuum device is noninvasive and inexpensive. Testosterone is indicated only for confirmed hypogonadism, and his level is normal. His exercise tolerance shows that sexual activity is safe from a cardiac standpoint.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Sildenafil 25 mg as needed — Sildenafil with a daily nitrate can cause life-threatening hypotension.
  • B. Daily tadalafil 5 mg — Tadalafil is long-acting and equally contraindicated with nitrates.
  • C. Testosterone replacement therapy — Testosterone treats only confirmed hypogonadism; his level is normal.
  • E. Sildenafil taken 24 hours after the last nitrate dose — No safe window exists for a man who takes a long-acting nitrate every day.

Key point

Avoid PDE-5 inhibitors in men taking nitrates and offer non-drug options for erectile dysfunction

Memory hook

Daily nitrate plus sildenafil equals crashing blood pressure: offer a vacuum device or alprostadil instead.
