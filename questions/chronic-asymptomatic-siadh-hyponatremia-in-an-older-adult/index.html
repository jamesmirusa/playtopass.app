USMLE Step 2 CK practice question: Chronic asymptomatic SIADH hyponatremia in an older adult: fluid restriction as first-line therapy

USMLE Step 2 CKRenalInternal Medicine

A 78-year-old woman comes to the physician for follow-up of a low serum sodium concentration found on routine testing 2 weeks ago. She feels well and has had no headache, confusion, falls, nausea or vomiting. She drinks about 2.5 L of water and tea daily because she read that it is healthy. She takes no diuretics, antidepressants, antiepileptics or other medications. Blood pressure is 134/82 mm Hg with no orthostatic change. She has moist mucous membranes, normal skin turgor and no edema. Serum studies show sodium 127 mEq/L (unchanged from 2 weeks ago), potassium 4.1 mEq/L, glucose 92 mg/dL, urea nitrogen 9 mg/dL, creatinine 0.7 mg/dL, uric acid 2.8 mg/dL, osmolality 262 mOsm/kg, TSH 1.9 μU/mL and morning cortisol 16 μg/dL. Urine osmolality is 480 mOsm/kg and urine sodium is 52 mEq/L. Chest CT and age-appropriate cancer screening are unremarkable.

Which of the following is the most appropriate initial management?

  1. Bolus of 3% hypertonic saline
  2. Restrict fluids to 800 mL daily
  3. Continuous 0.9% saline infusion
  4. Oral tolvaptan 15 mg daily
  5. Intranasal desmopressin
Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Restrict fluids to 800 mL daily

Euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia with inappropriately concentrated urine (osmolality above 100 mOsm/kg), urine sodium above 30 mEq/L, low uric acid and urea, and normal thyroid, adrenal and kidney function defines SIADH; in older adults it is often idiopathic once drugs, lung disease, central nervous system disorders and cancer are excluded. Because her hyponatremia is chronic, moderate and asymptomatic, the first-line treatment in both the US expert panel recommendations and the European guideline is fluid restriction, typically to about 800 to 1000 mL/day, which here also stops her deliberate excess water intake. A high ratio of urine electrolytes to serum sodium predicts that restriction will fail; second-line options then include oral urea, a loop diuretic with salt tablets or a vasopressin V2 receptor antagonist such as tolvaptan, which is costly and limited to 30 days because of liver injury. Hypertonic saline is for severe symptoms such as seizures or confusion. Isotonic saline can worsen SIADH because the kidney excretes the salt and keeps the water. Desmopressin is a vasopressin agonist and would lower sodium further.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Bolus of 3% hypertonic saline — Hypertonic saline boluses are reserved for severe or acutely symptomatic hyponatremia.
  • C. Continuous 0.9% saline infusion — Isotonic saline can lower sodium further in SIADH because the salt is excreted and the water retained.
  • D. Oral tolvaptan 15 mg daily — Tolvaptan is a second-line option with liver toxicity, high cost and a 30-day limit.
  • E. Intranasal desmopressin — Desmopressin is a vasopressin agonist and would worsen water retention.

Key point

Start fluid restriction as first-line treatment for chronic asymptomatic hyponatremia due to SIADH

Memory hook

Sodium 127, feels fine, concentrated urine, drinks 2.5 L a day for health: first restrict the water.
