USMLE for international medical graduates: the 2026 roadmap and costs

Updated: 2026-11-19

If you studied medicine outside the United States and Canada, you need ECFMG Certification before you can start a U.S. residency. This is the order most international medical graduates (IMGs) follow, with the costs you should budget for.

The path in seven steps

  1. Check your medical school. It must be listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools with a note that its students and graduates are eligible to apply to ECFMG.
  2. Create your MyIntealth account and apply for ECFMG Certification. Your identity and your medical diploma are verified as part of the process.
  3. Pass USMLE Step 1. Pass/fail, a basic-science exam set in clinical vignettes.
  4. Pass USMLE Step 2 CK. A three-digit score (218 to pass since July 2025) and the score programs compare.
  5. Meet the clinical and communication skills requirement. Since Step 2 CS ended, IMGs do this through one of the ECFMG Pathways, which include a satisfactory score on the Occupational English Test (OET) Medicine.
  6. Apply for residency. Applications go through ERAS and positions are filled through the NRMP Match. U.S. clinical experience and U.S. letters of recommendation strengthen an IMG application.
  7. Step 3. Usually taken during residency; some applicants take it earlier, for example when a visa type requires it.

ECFMG sets time limits and other rules for completing the exam requirements. Read the current ECFMG Information Booklet before you start.

Add preparation materials, travel to a test center if none is near you, and residency application fees. Many IMGs spend more on preparation and travel than on the exam fees themselves, so plan the whole budget up front.

Choosing where to test

When you apply for Step 1 or Step 2 CK you choose a testing region, then book any Prometric center in that region that offers the USMLE. Seat availability differs a lot by country. Check the Prometric site for current centers and dates before you pick your region; changing regions later costs a fee.

How long it takes

Most IMGs need 12–24 months from the first study day to Match day: several months for each Step, the Pathway and OET in parallel, then the application season. Starting Step 1 preparation while still in medical school shortens the path.
